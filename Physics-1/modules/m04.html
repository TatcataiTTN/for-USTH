Uniform circular motion

Speed is constant but the velocity turns, so there is a centripetal acceleration toward the centre.

Key formulas
$\omega=\dfrac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}=2\pi f$, $\ v=\omega r$, $\ a_c=\dfrac{v^2}{r}=\omega^2r$

How it is asked: Period and frequency; Speed of a point on a wheel.

Uniform circular motion: method and traps

Method
  1. Convert rpm to rad/s ($\times2\pi/60$) and degrees to radians.
  2. Link $T$, $f$, $\omega$, $v$ with $v=\omega r$.
  3. Centripetal acceleration points to the centre and has magnitude $v^2/r$.
Watch out
  • Centripetal acceleration is not zero even at constant speed.

Centripetal force: pendulum, vertical circle, banked curve

Centripetal force is the net force toward the centre, $F_c=mv^2/r$; it is supplied by tension, gravity, normal force or friction.

Key formulas
bottom of a pendulum: $T-mg=\dfrac{mv^2}{L}$; top of a vertical circle: $T+mg=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$, minimum $v_{top}=\sqrt{gr}$; flat curve: $\mu_smg=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

How it is asked: Tension at the bottom of a swing; Bucket of water in a vertical circle; Minimum speed to stay on the track.

Centripetal force: pendulum, vertical circle, banked curve: method and traps

Method
  1. Draw the forces at the instant asked and take the axis toward the centre.
  2. Write $\sum F_{radial}=mv^2/r$ with signs relative to the centre.
  3. Combine with energy conservation to get the speed at another point.
Watch out
  • ‘Centrifugal force’ is not a real force in an inertial frame.

Rotation of rigid bodies: torque, moment of inertia, rolling

Rotation obeys $\tau=I\alpha$; kinetic energy $\tfrac12I\omega^2$; rolling without slipping links $v=\omega R$.

Key formulas
$\tau=rF\sin\theta$, $\ \tau=I\alpha$, $\ K_{rot}=\tfrac12I\omega^2$, $\ L=I\omega$, solid sphere $\tfrac25MR^2$, disc $\tfrac12MR^2$, ring $MR^2$

How it is asked: Angular acceleration from a torque; Rolling down an incline.

Rotation of rigid bodies: torque, moment of inertia, rolling: method and traps

Method
  1. Pick the axis; compute torques with the lever arm.
  2. Write $\sum\tau=I\alpha$ and the translation equation if the body also moves.
  3. Rolling: use $a=\alpha R$ or energy $mgh=\tfrac12mv^2+\tfrac12I\omega^2$.
Watch out
  • Using the wrong $I$ for the shape or the axis.

Centripetal force against speed and radius

A $1000\,\mathrm{kg}$ car on a circular path: $F=mv^2/r$ grows with the square of the speed and falls with the radius. Friction (or banking) must supply it, which is why sharp bends are taken slowly.

$$a_c=\frac{v^2}{r},\qquad F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}$$

Ideal speed on a banked curve

On a frictionless banked bend the normal force alone supplies the centripetal force: $\tan\theta=v^2/(rg)$. The ideal speed grows with the bank angle and with the radius.

$$N\cos\theta=mg,\quad N\sin\theta=\frac{mv^2}{r}\ \Rightarrow\ v=\sqrt{rg\tan\theta}$$

Rotation with constant angular acceleration

$\alpha=2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, $\omega_0=1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, point at $r=0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. The tangential speed $v=\omega r$ grows linearly; the centripetal acceleration $\omega^2r$ grows quadratically; the tangential acceleration $\alpha r$ is constant.

$$\theta=\omega_0t+\tfrac12\alpha t^2,\quad \omega=\omega_0+\alpha t,\quad v=\omega r,\quad a_c=\omega^2r$$

Moment of inertia and the rolling race

$I=\beta MR^2$ with $\beta=1$ (hoop), $\tfrac23$ (hollow sphere), $\tfrac12$ (cylinder), $\tfrac25$ (solid sphere). Rolling down a $30^\circ$ incline, $a=g\sin\theta/(1+\beta)$: the smaller $\beta$, the faster; a frictionless sliding block beats them all.

$$a=\frac{g\sin\theta}{1+I/(MR^2)},\qquad K=\tfrac12Mv^2\,(1+\beta)$$

Conservation of angular momentum: the spinning skater

With no external torque $L=I\omega$ stays constant ($8\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$). Pulling the arms in lowers $I$ from $4$ to $1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$: $\omega$ rises from $2$ to $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the kinetic energy from $8$ to $32\,\mathrm{J}$ (the skater does the work).

$$L=I\omega=\text{const},\qquad K=\frac{L^2}{2I}$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Dynamics of circular motion (Mechanics tutorial Part 5, Q12)
A body is released from rest at height $h$ on a frictionless incline that leads into a vertical loop of radius $R$. What is the minimum $h$ for the body to complete the loop?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $2.5R$

At the top of the loop the normal force can be zero at best: $mg=\dfrac{mv^2}{R}\Rightarrow v^2=gR$. Energy from the release point to the top: $mgh=mg(2R)+\tfrac12mv^2=2mgR+\tfrac12mgR$, so $h=2.5R$.
Worked example 2 · Uniform circular motion (Mechanics tutorial Part 5, Q3)
A communication satellite moves at constant speed in a circular orbit around the Earth. Which row is correct for its momentum and kinetic energy?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: momentum changing, kinetic energy constant

Momentum $m\vec v$ is a vector: its direction keeps changing, so it is not constant. Kinetic energy $\tfrac12mv^2$ depends only on the (constant) speed.
Worked example 3 · Angular quantities (Mechanics tutorial Part 5, Q6)
What is the mean angular speed of the minute hand of a clock?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.0017\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

One revolution ($2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$) per $3600\,\mathrm{s}$: $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{3600}=1.7\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
Worked example 4 · Dynamics of circular motion (Mechanics tutorial Part 5, Q9)
A pendulum bob of mass $1.27\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from a string of length $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$. When the string is vertical the bob moves horizontally at $0.575\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the tension in the string? ($g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$)
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $13.2\,\mathrm{N}$

At the lowest point the net force is centripetal (upwards): $T-mg=\dfrac{mv^2}{L}$. $T=mg+\dfrac{mv^2}{L}=12.46+0.70=13.2\,\mathrm{N}$.
