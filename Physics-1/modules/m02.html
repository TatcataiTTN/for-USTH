Newton's laws of motion

(1) Without a net force a body keeps its velocity; (2) $\sum\vec F=m\vec a=d\vec p/dt$; (3) forces come in equal and opposite pairs acting on different bodies.

Key formulas
$\sum\vec F=m\vec a$, $\ \vec F_{AB}=-\vec F_{BA}$

How it is asked: Which statement is not a Newton law; What happens when the push stops.

Newton's laws of motion: method and traps

Method
  1. Identify the body and every force on it (not the forces it exerts).
  2. Net force zero ⇒ constant velocity (rest or uniform motion); non-zero ⇒ acceleration along the net force.
  3. Action–reaction pairs act on different bodies, so they never cancel.
Watch out
  • Constant speed does not need a net force; it needs a zero net force.
  • Conservation of momentum is a consequence, not one of the three laws.

Newton's second law and free-body diagrams

Draw the forces on one body, choose axes, write $\sum F_x=ma_x$ and $\sum F_y=ma_y$, solve.

Key formulas
$\sum F_x=ma_x$, $\ \sum F_y=ma_y$, weight $W=mg$, $\ F=\dfrac{dp}{dt}$

How it is asked: Net force from acceleration; Rocket sled; Girl and sled pulled by a rope.

Newton's second law and free-body diagrams: method and traps

Method
  1. Free-body diagram: weight, normal, tension, friction, applied forces.
  2. Choose axes along the acceleration; write the component equations.
  3. Solve for the unknown, then check units and the limiting case ($F=0$, $m\to\infty$).
Watch out
  • Putting the acceleration into the force list as if it were a force.
  • Mixing the forces on two different bodies in one equation.

Weight, normal force and apparent weight

Weight is $mg$; the scale reads the normal force $N$, which differs from $mg$ when the support accelerates.

Key formulas
lift up with $a$: $N=m(g+a)$; lift down with $a$: $N=m(g-a)$; free fall: $N=0$

How it is asked: Scale reading in a lift; Apparent weightlessness.

Weight, normal force and apparent weight: method and traps

Method
  1. Take the axis along the acceleration of the lift.
  2. Write $N-mg=ma$ (up positive) and isolate $N$.
  3. Compare with $mg$: more if accelerating upward, less if accelerating downward.
Watch out
  • Constant velocity in a lift gives the true weight.
  • ‘Descending’ does not mean ‘accelerating downward’.
  • Learning Support tip: in Physics I the only gravitational force you compute is the weight $mg$ ($g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$); the normal force is the perpendicular contact force, so on an incline it equals the perpendicular component of the weight when nothing else pushes or pulls across the surface.

Friction

Static friction adjusts up to $\mu_sN$; kinetic friction is $\mu_kN$ opposite to the sliding.

Key formulas
$f_s\le\mu_sN$, $\ f_k=\mu_kN$, on a flat floor $N=mg$

How it is asked: Box pushed at constant speed; Unicycle friction direction.

Friction: method and traps

Method
  1. Find $N$ from the perpendicular force balance.
  2. Static case: compare the applied force with $\mu_sN$; sliding: use $f_k=\mu_kN$.
  3. Put $f$ in the force equation along the motion with the correct sign.
Watch out
  • Friction on a driven wheel points forward.
  • Constant speed ⇒ push equals friction.
  • Learning Support tip: unless the statement says frictionless, include kinetic friction $\mu_kN$; static friction mostly appears in theory questions.

Inclined plane

Resolve weight into $mg\sin\theta$ along the plane and $mg\cos\theta$ perpendicular to it.

Key formulas
$a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)$, $\ N=mg\cos\theta$, $\ v^2=2g\sin\theta\,d$

How it is asked: Slide down an incline; Minimum angle before sliding.

Inclined plane: method and traps

Method
  1. Axes: $x$ along the incline, $y$ perpendicular.
  2. $N=mg\cos\theta$; down-slope force $mg\sin\theta$; friction up-slope.
  3. Solve $ma=mg\sin\theta-f$; use kinematics or energy for speeds.
Watch out
  • Sine and cosine swapped.

Connected bodies, tension and pulleys

Bodies joined by a taut light string share the same acceleration magnitude; tension is the same on both ends of an ideal string over an ideal pulley.

Key formulas
hanging mass + block on table: $a=\dfrac{m_2g-f}{m_1+m_2}$; Atwood: $a=\dfrac{(m_2-m_1)g}{m_1+m_2}$, $T=\dfrac{2m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$

How it is asked: Blocks pushed in contact; Three blocks pulled by a string; Hanging mass on a rough bench.

Connected bodies, tension and pulleys: method and traps

Method
  1. Treat the whole system along the string to get $a$.
  2. Isolate one body and write its equation to find the tension or the contact force.
  3. Check with the other body (Newton's third law).
Watch out
  • The tension in a string that pulls $k$ blocks behind it carries $k\,ma$.

Spring force (Hooke's law)

A spring pulls back with a force proportional to its extension, $F=-kx$; its stored energy is $\tfrac12kx^2$.

Key formulas
$F=-k\,\Delta x$, $\ U=\tfrac12kx^2$, $\ W=\tfrac12k(x_i^2-x_f^2)$

How it is asked: Spring launches a block; Work to stretch a spring.

Spring force (Hooke's law): method and traps

Method
  1. Find the extension from the natural length.
  2. Force: $F=k\Delta x$ (magnitude positive).
  3. Energy methods: $\tfrac12kx^2$ converts to kinetic or gravitational energy.
Watch out
  • The extension, not the total length, goes in Hooke's law.
  • Learning Support tip: if the question asks for the magnitude of the spring force, give the positive value $kx$ (absolute value), not the signed $-kx$.

Acceleration down an incline against the angle

$a=g(\sin\theta-\mu\cos\theta)$ once $\tan\theta>\mu$; below that angle friction holds the block. At $90^\circ$ every curve reaches $g$ (free fall).

$$a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta),\qquad \tan\theta_{\min}=\mu_s$$

Atwood machine: acceleration and tension against the mass ratio

$m_1=1\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2=r\,m_1$. For $r\to\infty$ the acceleration approaches $g$ and the tension approaches $2m_1g$; for $r=1$ nothing moves and $T=m_1g$.

$$a=\frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}\,g,\qquad T=\frac{2m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}\,g$$

Static and kinetic friction against the applied force

A $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ crate ($\mu_s=0.5$, $\mu_k=0.3$): friction equals the applied force until $\mu_smg=49\,\mathrm{N}$, then drops to $\mu_kmg=29.4\,\mathrm{N}$ and the crate accelerates.

$$f_s\le\mu_sN,\qquad f_k=\mu_kN,\qquad a=\frac{F-\mu_kmg}{m}$$

Hooke's law: single springs, springs in parallel and in series

$k_1=200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $k_2=300\,\mathrm{N/m}$. In parallel the constants add ($500$); in series the compliances add, so the combination is softer than either spring ($120$).

$$F=kx,\qquad k_{\parallel}=k_1+k_2,\qquad \frac1{k_{\rm series}}=\frac1{k_1}+\frac1{k_2}$$

Apparent weight in an elevator

A $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ passenger: $+1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $2\,\mathrm{s}$, cruise, then $-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $2\,\mathrm{s}$. The scale reads $N=m(g+a)$: more than $mg$ while speeding up, exactly $mg$ at constant speed, less while slowing down.

$$N-mg=ma\ \Rightarrow\ N=m(g+a)$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Newton's second law (Mechanics tutorial Part 3, Q16(b))
A $1000\,\mathrm{kg}$ car is towed from rest to $0.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by a rope with average tension $4000\,\mathrm{N}$. How far does the towed car travel while it speeds up?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.08\,\mathrm{m}$

Work-energy: $Fs=\tfrac12mv^2\Rightarrow s=\dfrac{\tfrac12(1000)(0.8)^2}{4000}=0.080\,\mathrm{m}$. Check with impulse: $t=\Delta p/F=0.20\,\mathrm{s}$, $s=\bar vt=0.4\times0.2=0.080\,\mathrm{m}$.
Worked example 2 · Pulleys and connected bodies (Mechanics tutorial Part 3, Q20(a, b))
A block $m_1=3.70\,\mathrm{kg}$ on a frictionless $30.0^\circ$ incline is connected by a cord over a frictionless pulley to a hanging block $m_2=2.30\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the blocks?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.735\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

Take down the slope and down for the hanging block as positive. Driving force: $m_2g-m_1g\sin\theta=22.54-18.13=4.41\,\mathrm{N}$. $a=\dfrac{4.41}{6.00}=0.735\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$; the hanging block accelerates DOWNWARDS (it wins).
Worked example 3 · Friction (Mechanics tutorial Part 3, Q24(a))
A $3.00\,\mathrm{kg}$ block slides from rest down a $30.0^\circ$ incline, moving $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ in $1.50\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its acceleration?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $1.78\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

From rest $s=\tfrac12at^2\Rightarrow a=\dfrac{2s}{t^2}=\dfrac{4.00}{2.25}=1.78\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
Worked example 4 · Newton's laws (Mechanics tutorial Part 3, Q4)
The graph of the force $F$ exerted on a body against time $t$ is given. The area under the graph represents the change of which quantity of the body?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: momentum

Impulse $=\int F\,\mathrm{d}t=\Delta p$: the area under a force-time graph is the change in momentum.
