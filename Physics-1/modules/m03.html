Work by constant and variable forces

Work is the energy transferred by a force along a displacement: $W=\vec F\cdot\vec d$ for a constant force, $\int F\,dx$ in general.

Key formulas
$W=Fd\cos\theta$, $\ W=\vec F\cdot\Delta\vec r$, $\ W=\int_{x_A}^{x_B}F(x)\,dx$, unit joule

How it is asked: Work by a force on a cart with a cord; Work from vectors.

Work by constant and variable forces: method and traps

Method
  1. Identify the force and the displacement vector $\Delta\vec r=\vec r_2-\vec r_1$.
  2. Constant force: dot product; variable force: integrate along the path.
  3. A force perpendicular to the motion (normal force, magnetic force) does no work.
Watch out
  • Using the whole force instead of the component along the displacement.

Kinetic energy and the work–energy theorem

The net work done on a body equals its change of kinetic energy.

Key formulas
$K=\tfrac12mv^2$, $\ W_{net}=\Delta K=K_f-K_i$

How it is asked: Speed after a pull; Change in kinetic energy of the cart.

Kinetic energy and the work–energy theorem: method and traps

Method
  1. Find all forces that do work along the path.
  2. Write $W_{net}=\tfrac12mv_f^2-\tfrac12mv_i^2$.
  3. Isolate the unknown speed, distance or force.
Watch out
  • Kinetic energy is a scalar and never negative.

Potential energy and conservation of mechanical energy

With only conservative forces $K+U$ is constant; gravitational $U=mgh$, elastic $U=\tfrac12kx^2$.

Key formulas
$K_i+U_i=K_f+U_f$ (plus $W_{nc}$ if friction acts), $\ mgh=\tfrac12mv^2$

How it is asked: Vertical loop minimum height; Speed at the bottom of a track.

Potential energy and conservation of mechanical energy: method and traps

Method
  1. Choose the zero level of $U$ and two instants (start, end).
  2. Write $E_i=E_f$ (add the work of friction if present).
  3. Solve for the speed or height; check the limiting case.
Watch out
  • Energy is conserved only if non-conservative forces do no work.

Conservative and non-conservative forces

A force is conservative if its work between two points does not depend on the path (gravity, spring); friction is not.

Key formulas
$W_{cons}=-\Delta U$; around a closed path $W_{cons}=0$

How it is asked: Is the force conservative.

Conservative and non-conservative forces: method and traps

Method
  1. Ask whether the work depends on the path.
  2. If yes (friction, drag) use $W_{nc}=\Delta E_{mech}$.
Watch out
  • Friction does negative work and turns mechanical energy into heat.

Power and efficiency

Power is the rate of doing work, $P=W/t=\vec F\cdot\vec v$; efficiency is useful output over input.

Key formulas
$P=\dfrac Wt=Fv$, $\ 1\ \mathrm{W}=1\ \mathrm{J/s}$, $\ \eta=\dfrac{E_{useful}}{E_{in}}$

How it is asked: Engine power and driving force; Light bulb efficiency.

Power and efficiency: method and traps

Method
  1. Convert kW to W and hours to seconds.
  2. Use $P=Fv$ at constant speed, or $P=W/t$ with the work from energy.
  3. Efficiency: divide useful by total.
Watch out
  • kilowatt-hour is an energy, not a power.

Momentum and impulse

Momentum $\vec p=m\vec v$; the impulse of the net force equals the change of momentum.

Key formulas
$\vec p=m\vec v$, $\ \vec F_{net}=\dfrac{d\vec p}{dt}$, $\ \vec J=\int\vec F\,dt=\Delta\vec p$

How it is asked: Force from impulse; Rate of change of momentum in free fall.

Momentum and impulse: method and traps

Method
  1. Pick the positive direction and write $p_i$ and $p_f$ with signs.
  2. Impulse $=F_{avg}\Delta t=\Delta p$.
  3. Free fall: $dp/dt=mg$, the weight.
Watch out
  • Reversal of velocity doubles the momentum change.

Collisions and conservation of momentum

Momentum is conserved in every collision of an isolated system; kinetic energy only in elastic ones; perfectly inelastic bodies stick together.

Key formulas
$m_1v_{1i}+m_2v_{2i}=m_1v_{1f}+m_2v_{2f}$, elastic: $v_{2f}=\dfrac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2}v_{1i}$ (target at rest), $e=\dfrac{|v_{2f}-v_{1f}|}{|v_{1i}-v_{2i}|}$, sticky: $v=\dfrac{m_1v_1+m_2v_2}{m_1+m_2}$

How it is asked: Skiers hugging in 2-D; Elastic head-on collision; Fraction of kinetic energy lost.

Collisions and conservation of momentum: method and traps

Method
  1. Define the system and the axes; momentum is a vector (2-D: each component).
  2. Always write conservation of momentum; add conservation of kinetic energy only for elastic.
  3. Solve; check that $K_f\le K_i$ for inelastic collisions.
Watch out
  • Kinetic energy is not conserved when the bodies stick.
  • Learning Support tip: total momentum is conserved in every collision; kinetic energy only when the collision is elastic.

Work as the area under the force-position graph

A force that rises to $3\,\mathrm{N}$ over the first $2\,\mathrm{m}$, stays constant to $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and falls to zero at $6\,\mathrm{m}$ does $3+6+3=12\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. The right panel is the running total $W(x)$.

$$W=\int_{x_1}^{x_2}F\,dx=\Delta K$$

A potential-energy curve: turning points and kinetic energy

For $U(x)=x^4/4-x^2$ (joules) and total energy $E=-0.6\,\mathrm{J}$ the particle can only be where $U\le E$; the kinetic energy $K=E-U$ is the gap between the line and the curve. The force is minus the slope of $U$.

$$K=E-U(x),\qquad F=-\frac{dU}{dx}$$

One-dimensional collisions against the mass ratio

$m_1=1\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ hits $m_2=r\,m_1$ at rest. Elastic: $m_1$ stops at $r=1$ and bounces back for $r>1$. Perfectly inelastic: the common speed is $u/(1+r)$ and the fraction $r/(1+r)$ of the kinetic energy is lost.

$$v_1'=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}u,\quad v_2'=\frac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2}u,\quad v'=\frac{m_1u}{m_1+m_2}$$

Impulse of a collision force

A half-sine force pulse of peak $600\,\mathrm{N}$ lasting $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ gives an impulse $2F_0T/\pi=3.82\,\mathrm{N\,s}$; on a $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ ball that is a velocity change of $25.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$J=\int F\,dt=\Delta p=m\,\Delta v$$

Power needed by a car against its speed

Resistance $F=200+0.4v^2$ (newtons). The power needed, $P=Fv$, grows like $v^3$ at high speed; with a $60\,\mathrm{kW}$ engine the top speed is where the curve crosses $60\,\mathrm{kW}$.

$$P=Fv=(f_0+cv^2)\,v$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Momentum and collisions (Mechanics tutorial Part 4, Q6)
A sphere of mass $m$ on a string of length $\ell$ is released from the horizontal position and strikes, at the bottom, a block of mass $4m$ at rest on a smooth table (perfectly elastic collision). The velocity of the block just after the collision is
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $\dfrac25\sqrt{2g\ell}$ in the direction of the sphere's motion

Swing: $mg\ell=\tfrac12mv^2\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{2g\ell}$. Elastic collision with $M=4m$: $v_M=\dfrac{2m}{m+4m}v=\dfrac25\sqrt{2g\ell}$ and the sphere rebounds with $\dfrac{m-4m}{5m}v=-\dfrac35\sqrt{2g\ell}$. Check: momentum $m v=m(-\tfrac35v)+4m(\tfrac25v)=m v$.
Worked example 2 · Work and energy (Mechanics tutorial Part 4, Q17)
A cord, pulled over a pulley $h=1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the cart's level, drags a cart along a frictionless rail from $x_1=3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $x_2=1.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The cord tension is a constant $25.0\,\mathrm{N}$. What is the change in the kinetic energy of the cart?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $41.7\,\mathrm{J}$

Work by the tension $=T\times$ (decrease in the length of cord from the pulley to the cart). Length: $\sqrt{x_1^2+h^2}=\sqrt{10.44}=3.231\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\sqrt{x_2^2+h^2}=\sqrt{2.44}=1.562\,\mathrm{m}$. $\Delta K=25.0(3.231-1.562)=41.7\,\mathrm{J}$. Using $T\Delta x=50\,\mathrm{J}$ would ignore that the force is not horizontal.
Worked example 3 · Potential energy and conservation (Mechanics tutorial Part 4, Q23(d))
The swing child ($25\,\mathrm{kg}$, released from a height $0.268\,\mathrm{m}$ above the lowest point) actually has speed $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the lowest position. What is the change in mechanical energy due to friction?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $-15.6\,\mathrm{J}$

Final mechanical energy $=\tfrac12(25)(2.00)^2=50.0\,\mathrm{J}$; initial $=65.6\,\mathrm{J}$. $\Delta E=50.0-65.6=-15.6\,\mathrm{J}$ (15.6 J lost as heat).
Worked example 4 · Power (Mechanics tutorial Part 4, Q31)
A body of mass $m$ moves up a frictionless track inclined at $\theta$ to the horizontal. What power is needed to give it a forward acceleration $a$ when its velocity is $v$?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $mav+mgv\sin\theta$

Force along the track: $F=ma+mg\sin\theta$. Power $=Fv=mav+mgv\sin\theta$.
