Ideal gas law

$pV=nRT=NkT$ with absolute pressure and kelvin; at fixed amount of gas $pV/T$ is constant.

Key formulas
$pV=nRT$, $\ pV=NkT$, $\ R=8.314\ \mathrm{J/mol\,K}$, $\ k=1.38\times10^{-23}$ J/K, $\ N=nN_A$, $\ 1\ \mathrm{atm}=1.013\times10^5$ Pa

How it is asked: Two containers connected by a valve; What does pV/kT represent.

Ideal gas law: method and traps

Method
  1. Convert to SI: Pa, m³, K.
  2. Choose $pV=nRT$ (moles) or $pV=NkT$ (molecules); for a change of state use $p_1V_1/T_1=p_2V_2/T_2$.
  3. Containers joined: conserve the total number of moles.
Watch out
  • $pV/kT$ counts molecules, $pV/RT$ counts moles.

Kinetic theory: pressure, rms speed and average energy

Pressure comes from molecular collisions; average translational energy depends only on $T$.

Key formulas
$K_{avg}=\tfrac32kT$, $\ v_{rms}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3kT}{m}}$, $\ p=\dfrac{nM}{3V}v^2_{rms}$, $E_{int}=\tfrac32nRT$ (monatomic)

How it is asked: rms speed of a gas; Average kinetic energy of a molecule.

Kinetic theory: pressure, rms speed and average energy: method and traps

Method
  1. Use the molar mass in kg/mol.
  2. Compare gases at the same $T$: lighter molecules are faster ($v\propto1/\sqrt M$).
  3. All ideal gases share the same $K_{avg}$ at the same $T$.
Watch out
  • $v_{rms}$ is not the average speed.

Maxwell speed distribution

Molecular speeds follow Maxwell's law; the three characteristic speeds satisfy $v_p<v_{avg}<v_{rms}$.

Key formulas
$v_p=\sqrt{\dfrac{2RT}{M}}$, $\ v_{avg}=\sqrt{\dfrac{8RT}{\pi M}}$, $\ v_{rms}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}}$ ; ratios $1:1.128:1.225$

How it is asked: Most probable speed equals rms speed at another temperature.

Maxwell speed distribution: method and traps

Method
  1. Write the two speeds mentioned for the same gas.
  2. Set them equal (different $T$) and isolate the ratio of temperatures or masses.
  3. Raising $T$ shifts the peak right and lowers it.
Watch out
  • Order $v_p<v_{avg}<v_{rms}$.

Mean free path

Average distance between collisions: $\lambda=\dfrac{kT}{\sqrt2\pi d^2p}\propto\dfrac Tp$.

Key formulas
$\lambda=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2\pi d^2(N/V)}$, $\ N/V=\dfrac p{kT}$

How it is asked: λ when p and T both double; Frequency whose sound wavelength equals λ.

Mean free path: method and traps

Method
  1. Get the number density $N/V=p/kT$ in SI units.
  2. Substitute into $\lambda=1/(\sqrt2\pi d^2N/V)$; diameter in metres.
  3. Scaling questions: $\lambda\propto T/p$ and $\propto1/d^2$.
Watch out
  • Converting cm to m for the molecular diameter.

Degrees of freedom and molar specific heats

Each active degree of freedom stores $\tfrac12kT$ per molecule: monatomic $f=3$, diatomic $f=5$ (rotation, no vibration).

Key formulas
$C_V=\tfrac f2R$, $\ C_p=C_V+R$, $\ \gamma=\dfrac{C_p}{C_V}$ ; mono $\tfrac32R,\tfrac52R,\tfrac53$; di $\tfrac52R,\tfrac72R,\tfrac75$; $Q=nC\Delta T$, $\Delta E_{int}=nC_V\Delta T$

How it is asked: Heat at constant pressure vs constant volume; Mixture of gases.

Degrees of freedom and molar specific heats: method and traps

Method
  1. Decide mono- or diatomic gas (or a mixture, weighted by moles).
  2. Heat at constant $p$ uses $C_p$, at constant $V$ uses $C_V$.
  3. $\Delta E_{int}=nC_V\Delta T$ holds for every process of an ideal gas.
Watch out
  • $C$ is per mole; units J/(mol·K), not J/K.

Adiabatic process of an ideal gas

No heat flows ($Q=0$): $pV^\gamma$ and $TV^{\gamma-1}$ stay constant; compression heats the gas.

Key formulas
$p_iV_i^\gamma=p_fV_f^\gamma$, $\ T_iV_i^{\gamma-1}=T_fV_f^{\gamma-1}$, $\ T_f=T_i\left(\dfrac{p_f}{p_i}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$, $\ W=-\Delta E_{int}$

How it is asked: Final temperature after adiabatic expansion; Work in adiabatic compression.

Adiabatic process of an ideal gas: method and traps

Method
  1. Find $\gamma$ from the gas type.
  2. Use $pV^\gamma$ for pressure/volume, $TV^{\gamma-1}$ or the pressure form for temperature (kelvin).
  3. Work: $W=\frac{p_iV_i-p_fV_f}{\gamma-1}$ or $-nC_V\Delta T$.
Watch out
  • Adiabatic free expansion does not follow $pV^\gamma$ ($T$ unchanged).

Maxwell speed distribution of nitrogen

$N_2$ ($M=28\,\mathrm{g/mol}$) at $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and $600\,\mathrm{K}$. The most probable speed is below the mean, which is below the rms speed: $v_p<v_{\rm avg}<v_{\rm rms}$. Raising $T$ shifts and flattens the curve; the area stays $1$.

$$f(v)=4\pi\left(\frac{M}{2\pi RT}\right)^{3/2}v^2e^{-Mv^2/2RT},\quad v_p=\sqrt{\tfrac{2RT}{M}},\ v_{\rm avg}=\sqrt{\tfrac{8RT}{\pi M}},\ v_{\rm rms}=\sqrt{\tfrac{3RT}{M}}$$

Isotherms and an adiabat of an ideal gas

One mole of a monatomic gas ($\gamma=5/3$). Along an isotherm $pV$ is constant; along an adiabat $pV^\gamma$ is constant, so the adiabat is steeper: at the crossing point its slope is $\gamma$ times the slope of the isotherm.

$$pV=nRT,\qquad pV^{\gamma}=\text{const},\qquad \gamma=\frac{C_p}{C_V}$$

Molar heat capacities and the number of degrees of freedom

$C_V=\tfrac f2R$ and $C_p=C_V+R$: monatomic gases ($f=3$) have $\gamma=5/3$, diatomic gases at room temperature ($f=5$) $\gamma=7/5$, polyatomic gases ($f=6$) $\gamma=4/3$.

$$C_V=\frac f2R,\qquad C_p=C_V+R,\qquad \gamma=\frac{C_p}{C_V}=1+\frac2f$$

Mean free path of nitrogen against pressure

$\lambda=kT/(\sqrt2\,\pi d^2p)$ with $d=3.7\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}$ at $300\,\mathrm{K}$: inversely proportional to the pressure (slope $-1$ on log-log axes), about $67\,\mathrm{nm}$ at one atmosphere and millimetres in a rough vacuum.

$$\lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi d^2\,N/V}=\frac{kT}{\sqrt2\,\pi d^2p}$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Ideal gas law (Thermodynamics tutorial Part II (1), Q7)
An air bubble of volume $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ is at the bottom of a lake $40\,\mathrm{m}$ deep, where the temperature is $4.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$. It rises to the surface, at $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$, with the same temperature as the water. What is its volume at the surface?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $100\,\mathrm{cm^3}$

Pressure at depth: $p_1=p_0+\rho gh=1.013\times10^5+(1000)(9.8)(40)=4.93\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. $V_2=V_1\dfrac{p_1}{p_2}\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}=20\times\dfrac{4.93}{1.013}\times\dfrac{293.15}{277.15}=1.0\times10^2\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.
Worked example 2 · Molecular speeds (Thermodynamics tutorial Part II (1), Q10(a))
At $273\,\mathrm{K}$ and $1.00\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{atm}$ the density of a gas is $1.24\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the rms speed of its molecules?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $495\,\mathrm{m/s}$

$p=\tfrac13\rho v_{\text{rms}}^2\Rightarrow v_{\text{rms}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3p}{\rho}}$ with $p=1.013\times10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $\rho=1.24\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. $v_{\text{rms}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3(1013)}{0.0124}}=495\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
Worked example 3 · Mean free path (Thermodynamics tutorial Part II (1), Q13)
What is the mean free path of oxygen molecules (diameter $3.0\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{cm}$) at $1.0\,\mathrm{atm}$ and $0^\circ\mathrm{C}$?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $9.3\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}$

$\dfrac NV=\dfrac{p}{kT}=2.69\times10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$. $\lambda=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi d^2(N/V)}=\dfrac{1}{1.414\pi(3.0\times10^{-10})^2(2.69\times10^{25})}=9.3\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}$.
Worked example 4 · Heat capacities (Thermodynamics tutorial Part II (1), MCQ 8)
$70\,\mathrm{cal}$ of heat raises the temperature of $2$ moles of an ideal diatomic gas by $5^\circ\mathrm{C}$ at constant pressure. How much heat would raise the temperature of the same gas through the same range at constant volume?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $50\,\mathrm{cal}$

$\dfrac{Q_V}{Q_p}=\dfrac{C_V}{C_p}=\dfrac{5/2}{7/2}=\dfrac57$ for a diatomic gas (rotation but no vibration). $Q_V=70\times\dfrac57=50\,\mathrm{cal}$.
