Entropy and the second law

$\Delta S=\int dQ/T$ is a state function; for a closed system $\Delta S\ge0$ (zero only for reversible processes).

Key formulas
isothermal: $\Delta S=\dfrac QT$; heating: $\Delta S=mc\ln\dfrac{T_f}{T_i}$; ideal gas: $\Delta S=nR\ln\dfrac{V_f}{V_i}+nC_V\ln\dfrac{T_f}{T_i}$; phase change: $\dfrac{mL}{T}$

How it is asked: Heat from entropy change; Specific heat from ΔS graph.

Entropy and the second law: method and traps

Method
  1. Replace an irreversible process by any reversible one with the same end states.
  2. Pick the right formula (isothermal, heating, gas, phase change) and use kelvin.
  3. Sum over all bodies: $\Delta S_{total}\ge0$.
Watch out
  • Entropy of the universe never decreases, but a part of it can.

Heat engines and efficiency

A heat engine takes $|Q_H|$, does work $W$ and rejects $|Q_L|$: $W=|Q_H|-|Q_L|$, $\eta=W/|Q_H|$.

Key formulas
$\eta=\dfrac{W}{|Q_H|}=1-\dfrac{|Q_L|}{|Q_H|}$, per cycle $\Delta E_{int}=0$

How it is asked: Efficiency from a cycle; Heat rejected per cycle.

Heat engines and efficiency: method and traps

Method
  1. Per cycle: $W=Q_{net}$.
  2. Identify $Q_H$ (heat in) and $Q_L$ (heat out).
  3. Efficiency is below $100\%$ (second law).
Watch out
  • Use magnitudes $|Q|$ for efficiency formulas.

Carnot engine and cycle

The reversible engine between $T_H$ and $T_L$ (two isotherms, two adiabats) has the maximum efficiency $1-T_L/T_H$.

Key formulas
$\eta_C=1-\dfrac{T_L}{T_H}$, $\ \dfrac{|Q_H|}{T_H}=\dfrac{|Q_L|}{T_L}$ (absolute temperatures)

How it is asked: Raise T_H to reach 50%; Carnot cycle on a p–V diagram.

Carnot engine and cycle: method and traps

Method
  1. Convert both reservoir temperatures to kelvin.
  2. Isolate the unknown temperature from $\eta=1-T_L/T_H$.
  3. Use $Q_L/Q_H=T_L/T_H$ for the heat flows.
Watch out
  • The numbers $483$ K and $580$ K are temperatures, not the increase.

Refrigerators, heat pumps and COP

A refrigerator uses work $W$ to move heat $|Q_L|$ from cold to hot; $K=|Q_L|/W$ (Carnot: $K=T_L/(T_H-T_L)$).

Key formulas
$K=\dfrac{|Q_L|}{|W|}$, $\ |Q_H|=|Q_L|+|W|$, $\ K_C=\dfrac{T_L}{T_H-T_L}$, heat pump $K_{hp}=\dfrac{|Q_H|}{W}$

How it is asked: Air conditioner power; COP of a Carnot refrigerator.

Refrigerators, heat pumps and COP: method and traps

Method
  1. Heat to remove per second: $\dot m c\Delta T$.
  2. Best-case COP is the Carnot value (kelvin).
  3. Power needed $=\dot Q_L/K$.
Watch out
  • The rate of heat removed is not the electrical power.

Efficiency of a Carnot engine

$\eta=1-T_c/T_h$ depends only on the two reservoir temperatures: it falls linearly with $T_c/T_h$, and for $T_c=300\,\mathrm{K}$ it rises with $T_h$ but can never reach $1$. For $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$ it is $40\,\%$.

$$\eta_C=1-\frac{T_c}{T_h}=\frac{W}{Q_h}$$

The Carnot cycle on a p-V diagram

One mole of a monatomic gas between $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$: isothermal expansion A→B (heat $Q_h$ in), adiabatic expansion B→C, isothermal compression C→D (heat $Q_c$ out), adiabatic compression D→A. The enclosed area is the work, and $W/Q_h=1-T_c/T_h=0.40$.

$$Q_h=nRT_h\ln\frac{V_B}{V_A},\quad Q_c=nRT_c\ln\frac{V_C}{V_D},\quad W=Q_h-Q_c$$

Entropy change when water is heated

Heating $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water from $280$ to $360\,\mathrm{K}$: $\Delta S=mc\ln(T_2/T_1)=1052\,\mathrm{J/K}$. On the $T$-$S$ diagram the area under the curve is the heat absorbed, $mc\,\Delta T=335\,\mathrm{kJ}$.

$$\Delta S=\int\frac{dQ}{T}=mc\ln\frac{T_2}{T_1},\qquad Q=\int T\,dS$$

Refrigerator and heat-pump coefficients of performance

For a Carnot refrigerator $K=T_c/(T_h-T_c)$ and for a heat pump $K=T_h/(T_h-T_c)=K_{\rm fridge}+1$ (room at $T_h=300\,\mathrm{K}$). Both blow up as $T_c\to T_h$: moving heat across a small temperature difference is cheap.

$$K_{\rm fridge}=\frac{Q_c}{W}=\frac{T_c}{T_h-T_c},\qquad K_{\rm pump}=\frac{Q_h}{W}=\frac{T_h}{T_h-T_c}$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Entropy (Thermodynamics tutorial Part III, Q4(b))
For that copper block (heated reversibly from $298.15\,\mathrm{K}$ to $373.15\,\mathrm{K}$), what is the entropy change?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $173\,\mathrm{J/K}$

$\mathrm{d}S=\dfrac{mc\,\mathrm{d}T}{T}$, so $\Delta S=mc\ln\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}=772\ln\dfrac{373.15}{298.15}=173\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Not $Q/T_{\text{one}}$: the temperature changes during the heating (the answer lies between $Q/T_2=155$ and $Q/T_1=194\,\mathrm{J/K}$). Numerical integration of $\mathrm{d}Q/T$ agrees.
Worked example 2 · Carnot engine (Thermodynamics tutorial Part III, Q10)
A Carnot engine whose low-temperature reservoir is at $17^\circ\mathrm{C}$ has an efficiency of $40\%$. By how much must the temperature of the high-temperature reservoir be increased to raise the efficiency to $50\%$?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $96.7\,\mathrm{K}$

$\varepsilon=1-\dfrac{T_L}{T_H}\Rightarrow T_H=\dfrac{T_L}{1-\varepsilon}$ with $T_L=290.15\,\mathrm{K}$. $T_H=\dfrac{290.15}{0.60}=483.6\,\mathrm{K}$ at $40\%$ and $\dfrac{290.15}{0.50}=580.3\,\mathrm{K}$ at $50\%$: increase $=96.7\,\mathrm{K}$.
Worked example 3 · Heat engines (Thermodynamics tutorial Part III, Q16(a))
In the Otto-cycle model of a gasoline engine (compression ratio $4.00$, $\gamma=1.30$), what is the ratio $T_2/T_1$ of the temperatures at the end and the start of the adiabatic compression?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $1.52$

Adiabatic: $TV^{\gamma-1}=\text{const}$, so $\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}=\left(\dfrac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{\gamma-1}=4.00^{0.30}=1.52$.
Worked example 4 · Refrigerators and heat pumps (Thermodynamics tutorial Part III, Q17(a))
How much work must a Carnot refrigerator do to transfer $1.0\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat from a reservoir at 7 °C to one at 27 °C?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.0714\,\mathrm{J}$

$K=\dfrac{Q_L}{W}=\dfrac{T_L}{T_H-T_L}\Rightarrow W=Q_L\,\dfrac{T_H-T_L}{T_L}=\dfrac{300.15-280.15}{280.15}=0.0714\,\mathrm{J}$ (kelvins). The colder the cold reservoir, the more work each joule costs.
