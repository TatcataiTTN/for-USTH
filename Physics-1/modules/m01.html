Distance, displacement, speed and velocity

Displacement is the signed change of position; distance is the path length. Average velocity = displacement/time, average speed = distance/time.

Key formulas
$\bar v=\dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$, $\ \bar s=\dfrac{d}{\Delta t}$, $\ v=\dfrac{dx}{dt}$, $1\ \mathrm{km/h}=\dfrac1{3.6}\ \mathrm{m/s}$

How it is asked: Rank paths by average speed; Bird and two trains; Unit conversion km/h ↔ m/s.

Distance, displacement, speed and velocity: method and traps

Method
  1. Convert everything to SI (m, s) first.
  2. Decide whether the question asks for a vector quantity (displacement, velocity) or a scalar one (distance, speed).
  3. Split a round trip or a back-and-forth motion into legs, add distances, add signed displacements.
Watch out
  • Average speed ≠ magnitude of average velocity (round trip: velocity 0).
  • Average speed over two equal distances is the harmonic mean, not the arithmetic mean.

Acceleration and the constant-acceleration equations

For constant acceleration $a$: velocity changes linearly, position quadratically. Pick the equation that lacks the unknown.

Key formulas
$v=v_0+at$, $\ x=x_0+v_0t+\tfrac12at^2$, $\ v^2=v_0^2+2a\Delta x$, $\ \Delta x=\tfrac12(v_0+v)t$

How it is asked: Stopping distance; Two bodies meeting or chasing; Average speed with constant deceleration (speed trap).

Acceleration and the constant-acceleration equations: method and traps

Method
  1. List the five quantities $x,\ v_0,\ v,\ a,\ t$; mark the three that are given and the one asked for.
  2. Choose the equation that contains exactly those four and no fifth one.
  3. Isolate the unknown, substitute SI values, and check the sign (deceleration is negative along the motion).
Watch out
  • Using a non-constant $a$ in these formulas.
  • Taking a quadratic's negative root for a time.

Motion graphs: slope and area

On $x$–$t$ the slope is velocity; on $v$–$t$ the slope is acceleration and the signed area is displacement; on $a$–$t$ the area is the change of velocity.

Key formulas
$v=\dfrac{dx}{dt}$, $\ a=\dfrac{dv}{dt}$, $\ \Delta x=\int v\,dt$, $\ \Delta v=\int a\,dt$

How it is asked: Average velocity from a $v$–$t$ graph; Which interval has constant speed; Direction of travel from the sign of $v$.

Motion graphs: slope and area: method and traps

Method
  1. Say what the axes are and read the quantity asked for as a slope or as an area.
  2. Split the graph at zero crossings of $v$ to separate distance (absolute areas) from displacement (signed sum).
  3. For a straight $v$–$t$ line use $\bar v=\frac{v_i+v_f}{2}$ as a check.
Watch out
  • Area below the axis counts negative for displacement but positive for distance.
  • $a=0$ means constant velocity, not zero velocity.

Free fall and air resistance

Without air resistance every body falls with $g\approx9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ downward. With drag the acceleration shrinks until it reaches zero at the terminal speed.

Key formulas
$v=gt$, $\ h=\tfrac12gt^2$, $\ v=\sqrt{2gh}$, up and down are symmetric; apex $t=v_0/g$, $h_{max}=v_0^2/2g$

How it is asked: Time to fall the last 10 m; Rock passes a tower top; v–t graph in vacuum vs air.

Free fall and air resistance: method and traps

Method
  1. Choose the axis direction (up positive: $a=-g$).
  2. Use symmetry: time up = time down, speed at a height is the same going up and down.
  3. For the last part of a fall subtract two fall-from-rest times.
Watch out
  • ‘Heavier falls faster’ is false without air.
  • At the top $v=0$ but $a=-g$.

Relative motion

Velocities add as vectors through an intermediate frame: $\vec v_{13}=\vec v_{12}+\vec v_{23}$.

Key formulas
$\vec v_{BE}=\vec v_{BW}+\vec v_{WE}$ (boat–earth = boat–water + water–earth), relative closing speed $=v_1+v_2$ for head-on motion

How it is asked: Boat crossing a river; Two vehicles approaching; Rain seen from a moving car.

Relative motion: method and traps

Method
  1. Name the three bodies (object, intermediate frame, observer) and write $\vec v_{13}=\vec v_{12}+\vec v_{23}$ with matching subscripts.
  2. Resolve into $x$ and $y$ components.
  3. Recombine: magnitude by Pythagoras, direction by $\tan^{-1}$.
Watch out
  • Mixing up the order of subscripts.

Vectors: components, dot product, magnitude

A vector has magnitude and direction; add component by component; the dot product gives a scalar, the cross product a vector.

Key formulas
$\vec A=A_x\hat i+A_y\hat j+A_z\hat k$, $\ |\vec A|=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2+A_z^2}$, $\ \vec A\cdot\vec B=AB\cos\theta$

How it is asked: Resultant of displacements; Work as $\vec F\cdot\vec d$.

Vectors: components, dot product, magnitude: method and traps

Method
  1. Write each vector in components.
  2. Add or subtract componentwise; for work or projections use the dot product.
  3. Magnitude by Pythagoras, angle by $\tan^{-1}(A_y/A_x)$ with the correct quadrant.
Watch out
  • Using the wrong quadrant for the angle.

Projectile motion

Horizontal motion is uniform ($a_x=0$); vertical motion has $a_y=-g$. The two are independent and share only the time.

Key formulas
$v_x=v_0\cos\theta$, $\ v_y=v_0\sin\theta-gt$, $\ H=\dfrac{v_0^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$, $\ R=\dfrac{v_0^2\sin2\theta}{g}$, $\ T=\dfrac{2v_0\sin\theta}{g}$

How it is asked: Launch from a cliff; Time of flight, range, maximum height; Compare two projectiles.

Projectile motion: method and traps

Method
  1. Resolve the initial velocity into $v_{0x}$ and $v_{0y}$.
  2. Solve the vertical equation for the time (quadratic if launched from a height; keep the positive root).
  3. Use that time in $x=v_{0x}t$ for the range; use $v_y=0$ at the top for the maximum height.
Watch out
  • At the top only $v_y=0$; $v_x$, momentum and kinetic energy are not zero.
  • $R$ is maximal at $45^\circ$; complementary angles give equal ranges.

Constant acceleration: position, velocity, acceleration

A body with $v_0=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $a=1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The slope of the $x$-$t$ curve is $v$, the slope of the $v$-$t$ line is $a$ and the area under the $v$-$t$ line is the displacement.

$$x=x_0+v_0t+\tfrac12at^2,\quad v=v_0+at$$

Projectile motion: trajectories for five launch angles

Launch speed $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, no air resistance. Complementary angles ($15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$, $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$) give the same range; the maximum range is at $45^\circ$.

$$R=\frac{v_0^2\sin2\theta}{g},\quad H=\frac{v_0^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$$

Free fall with and without air resistance

Linear drag $F=-bv$ with terminal speed $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$: the speed approaches $v_t$ exponentially with time constant $\tau=v_t/g$, while without drag it grows without limit.

$$v=v_t\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau=\frac{v_t}{g},\quad v_t=\frac{mg}{b}$$

Reading motion graphs: a(t), v(t) and x(t) of one trip

Acceleration $+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $3\,\mathrm{s}$, then $0$ for $3\,\mathrm{s}$, then $-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $4\,\mathrm{s}$. The area under $a$-$t$ is the change of $v$; the area under $v$-$t$ is the change of $x$; the body stops at $t=10\,\mathrm{s}$ after $39\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$\Delta v=\int a\,dt,\qquad \Delta x=\int v\,dt$$

Adding and subtracting vectors by components

$\vec A=(3,4)$ and $\vec B=(-1,2)$: add or subtract component by component; the magnitude of the sum is $\sqrt{40}=6.32$, not $|A|+|B|=7.24$.

$$\vec A+\vec B=(A_x+B_x,\ A_y+B_y),\quad |\vec A|=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Free fall (Mechanics tutorial Part 1, Q4)
A metal ball hits a sand bed one second after it is released from rest and makes an impression $8.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ deep. Air resistance is negligible. What is the average deceleration of the ball in the sand?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $6000\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

Impact speed after $1\,\mathrm{s}$ of free fall: $v=gt=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the sand $v^2=2ad$ with $d=8.0\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$: $a=\dfrac{9.8^2}{2\times0.008}=6.0\times10^3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Forgetting the factor 2 gives $1.2\times10^4$.
Worked example 2 · Uniform acceleration (Mechanics tutorial Part 2, Q20)
In a speed trap two pressure strips are $120\,\mathrm{m}$ apart on a road with a limit of $85\,\mathrm{km/h}$. A driver at $110\,\mathrm{km/h}$ on the first strip slows down at once. What constant deceleration is needed so that his average speed between the strips just equals the limit?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $2.73\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

Convert: $110\,\mathrm{km/h}=30.56\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $85\,\mathrm{km/h}=23.61\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Time between strips at the limit: $t=120/23.61=5.08\,\mathrm{s}$. For constant deceleration $\bar v=(v_1+v_2)/2=23.61$, so $v_2=16.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$. $a=\dfrac{v_1-v_2}{t}=\dfrac{13.89}{5.08}=2.73\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Check: $\dfrac{v_1^2-v_2^2}{2d}=2.73$.
Worked example 3 · Projectile motion (Mechanics tutorial Part 2, Q24)
A cannon on a $50\,\mathrm{m}$ high cliff fires a ball over the edge at $200\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. How long is the ball in the air? (Neglect air resistance.)
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $20.9\,\mathrm{s}$

Vertical motion (up positive): $y=h_0+v_0\sin\theta\,t-\tfrac12gt^2=0$, i.e. $50+100t-4.9t^2=0$. Positive root: $t=\dfrac{100+\sqrt{100^2+4(4.9)(50)}}{2(4.9)}=20.9\,\mathrm{s}$. $2v_0\sin\theta/g=20.4\,\mathrm{s}$ is the time for a launch from ground level: the extra $50\,\mathrm{m}$ of height adds time.
Worked example 4 · Relative motion (Mechanics tutorial Part 2, Q25(a))
A car travels due east at $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Rain falls vertically relative to the Earth, but the traces on the side windows make $60.0^\circ$ with the vertical. What is the speed of the rain relative to the car?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $57.7\,\mathrm{km/h}$

In the car's frame the rain has a horizontal component $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$ (westwards) and the vertical component $v_y$ of the rain relative to Earth. The streaks make $60^\circ$ with the vertical, so $\sin60^\circ=\dfrac{50}{v_{rain/car}}$. $v_{rain/car}=\dfrac{50}{\sin60^\circ}=57.7\,\mathrm{km/h}$.
