How to train with this site

  1. Learn the chapter from your lecture slides and the textbook sections listed in the Sources page.
  2. Quiz it: do the three sets of that chapter. Read every explanation, also for the questions you got right. Use "Redo only the mistakes".
  3. Go deeper in the practice bank: tick written mode to hide the options, solve on paper, then compare with the model answer.
  4. Simulate the exams: the mock midterm (no going back), the 40-question mock final, then the 30-question / 20-minute speed exam. Review every explanation afterwards, also for the questions you got right.
  5. Repeat the weak chapters shown in the progress panel above until each is above 75%.

About the course (Course advice sheet by Pham Hieu Dung (AY 2025–26))

ComponentWeightDetails
Attendance10%lecture and tutor class; you are barred from the final if you miss more than 30% of sessions without a valid reason
Midterm30%MCQ test on Moodle, 20 questions (the documents do not give the duration; the mocks here use 40 minutes, our assumption)
Final60%MCQ test on Moodle, 40 questions (duration not stated; the mocks use 80 minutes, our assumption)
  • Content: Mechanics (kinematics, Newton's laws of motion, conservation of energy and momentum, circular motion) and Thermodynamics (zeroth, first and second laws, ideal gases, kinetic theory, entropy).
  • Bonus points are given generously in lectures and tutor classes: up to 4 points can be added directly to the final exam (the method differs per tutor lecturer).
  • The form of the test varies from year to year (multiple choice or constructed response, or both) and a large share of the midterm and final questions come from the tutor problems, so work through them all.
  • Thermodynamics appears a lot in the final and is harder than Mechanics; for Mechanics practise the Newton's-law and force problems.

Unofficial self-study material prepared from the USTH Fundamental Physics I course documents (not endorsed by USTH). Problem statements are paraphrased with their source cited on every question; solutions, explanations and checks are our own. Every numerical answer is computed by program from the physical law and re-derived by a second, independent route (another formula, energy instead of kinematics, numerical integration or simulation, ray-transfer matrices); differences from the printed answers of the course sheets are listed on the Sources page. Progress is stored only in your browser.
