Errors and oddities found in the course documents

Each item below was found by checking our computed answer against the printed one (or when an exercise turned out to be impossible as printed). The quiz uses the computed result.

  • Mechanics tutorial Part 2, Q12: option C is mistyped (it reads 'tan theta2 = sqrt2 tan theta2', theta2 on both sides); the relation that follows from h1 = 2 h2 is sin theta1 = sqrt2 sin theta2 (option B).
  • Thermodynamics tutorial Part II (1), Q1: the pressures are printed as 1.01x10^-5 Pa and 1.06x10^-5 Pa; the textbook problem has 10^5 Pa (about one atmosphere), which is what the questions use. The final temperature does not depend on the exponent.
  • Thermodynamics tutorial Part II (1), Q15: the mean free paths of argon and nitrogen are printed as 9.9x10^6 cm and 27.5x10^6 cm; the textbook values are 9.9x10^-6 cm and 27.5x10^-6 cm (a mean free path of 99 km at atmospheric pressure would be absurd). The ratio asked for is unaffected.
  • Thermodynamics tutorial Part II (2), Q1: the molecular diameters are printed as 1.0x10^8 cm and 3.0x10^8 cm; the textbook values are 1.0x10^-8 cm and 3.0x10^-8 cm (the minus sign was lost).

How answers were verified

  • Every correct answer is produced by code from the physical law (never typed by hand) and re-derived by a second, independent route before the question is kept: another formula (energy instead of kinematics, momentum instead of forces), root finding, numerical integration, a numerical simulation of the motion, or ray-transfer matrices. The build stops when the two routes disagree.
  • For problems taken from the course sheets the numbers of the sheet are used. Where the printed statement depends on a figure we could not reproduce, the setting was redrawn with our own data and the question is labelled generated, same style.
  • Distractors are the realistic mistakes (forgotten factor 2, sin and cos swapped, centimetres not converted, Celsius used instead of kelvin, wrong sign of the work, mass forgotten ...); every option of a question differs from the others at the displayed precision.
  • Option positions are shuffled with a fixed seed and balanced (A/B/C/D each 25%); the correct option is not systematically the longest.
University of Science and Technology of Hanoi · B1 · Fundamental Physics I (PHY1.001)

Unofficial self-study material prepared from the USTH Fundamental Physics I course documents (not endorsed by USTH). Problem statements are paraphrased with their source cited on every question; solutions, explanations and checks are our own. Every numerical answer is computed by program from the physical law and re-derived by a second, independent route (another formula, energy instead of kinematics, numerical integration or simulation, ray-transfer matrices); differences from the printed answers of the course sheets are listed on the Sources page. Progress is stored only in your browser.
