Plane and spherical mirrors

$\frac1s+\frac1{s'}=\frac1f$ with $f=R/2$; $f>0$ concave, $f<0$ convex; $m=-s'/s$.

Key formulas
$\dfrac1s+\dfrac1{s'}=\dfrac1f$, $\ f=\dfrac R2$, $\ m=-\dfrac{s'}{s}=\dfrac{h'}{h}$; convex mirror: always virtual, upright, reduced

How it is asked: Object distance for a given magnification; Image in a concave mirror.

Plane and spherical mirrors: method and traps

Method
  1. Fix the sign convention ($f<0$ for convex, $s'<0$ for virtual).
  2. Isolate $s'$ (or $s$) from the mirror equation; then $m$.
  3. Interpret: sign of $s'$ (real/virtual), sign of $m$ (upright/inverted), $|m|$ vs 1.
Watch out
  • Half-size image in a convex mirror is at $s=|f|$.

Refraction at a spherical surface

$\dfrac{n_1}{s}+\dfrac{n_2}{s'}=\dfrac{n_2-n_1}{R}$ with $R>0$ when the centre of curvature is on the outgoing side.

Key formulas
$\dfrac{n_1}{s}+\dfrac{n_2}{s'}=\dfrac{n_2-n_1}{R}$, $\ m=-\dfrac{n_1s'}{n_2s}$

How it is asked: Fish bowl / apparent depth; Glass sphere.

Refraction at a spherical surface: method and traps

Method
  1. Which medium holds the object ($n_1$) and the image side ($n_2$).
  2. Sign of $R$ from the position of the centre of curvature.
  3. Isolate $s'$.
Watch out
  • Apparent depth of an object under water ≈ real depth / n.

Thin lenses and the lensmaker's equation

$\frac1s+\frac1{s'}=\frac1f$ and $\frac1f=(n-1)\left(\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2}\right)$; converging $f>0$, diverging $f<0$.

Key formulas
$\dfrac1s+\dfrac1{s'}=\dfrac1f$, $\ m=-\dfrac{s'}{s}$, $\ P=\dfrac1f$ (dioptre), lenses in contact: $\dfrac1f=\dfrac1{f_1}+\dfrac1{f_2}$, two lenses: image of the first is the object of the second

How it is asked: Image position and magnification; Focal length from the radii; Two lenses in a row.

Thin lenses and the lensmaker's equation: method and traps

Method
  1. Isolate $s'$ from the thin-lens equation; then $m$.
  2. Object beyond $2f$: real, inverted, reduced; between $f$ and $2f$: real, inverted, enlarged; inside $f$: virtual, upright, enlarged.
  3. Two lenses: do them one after the other with the correct distances.
Watch out
  • Signs: virtual image $s'<0$, diverging lens $f<0$.

Thin lens: image distance and magnification against object distance

$f=10\,\mathrm{cm}$ converging lens. Object beyond $f$: a real inverted image ($s'>0$, $m<0$), at infinity when $s\to f^+$ and at $2f$ when $s=2f$ (then $m=-1$). Object inside $f$: a virtual, upright, enlarged image ($s'<0$, $m>1$).

$$\frac1s+\frac1{s'}=\frac1f,\qquad m=-\frac{s'}{s}$$

Ray diagram of a converging lens

Object $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ high, $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ from a lens with $f=10\,\mathrm{cm}$. The three principal rays (parallel → through the far focus; through the centre → straight; through the near focus → parallel) meet at $s'=16.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ giving an inverted image $m=-0.67$ ($2\,\mathrm{cm}$ high).

$$s'=\frac{sf}{s-f}=16.7\ \mathrm{cm},\qquad m=-\frac{s'}{s}=-0.667$$

Spherical mirrors: image distance against object distance

A concave mirror with $f=15\,\mathrm{cm}$ forms real images beyond $f$ (inverted) and virtual ones inside $f$; a convex mirror ($f=-15\,\mathrm{cm}$) always gives a virtual, upright, reduced image between the mirror and its focus.

$$\frac1s+\frac1{s'}=\frac1f=\frac2R,\qquad m=-\frac{s'}{s}$$

Lensmaker's equation: focal length of a biconvex lens

For a thin lens in air $1/f=(n-1)(1/R_1-1/R_2)$. Left: a symmetric biconvex lens with $n=1.5$ has $f=R$. Right: for $R_1=-R_2=20\,\mathrm{cm}$ a higher refractive index gives a shorter focal length.

$$\frac1f=(n-1)\left(\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2}\right)$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Plane and spherical mirrors (Tutorial chapter 34, 34.8)
A convex mirror has a radius of curvature of $3.00\,\mathrm{cm}$. An object is $18.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in front of it. Where is the image? (Negative: behind the mirror.)
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $-1.38\,\mathrm{cm}$

A convex mirror has a NEGATIVE focal length: $f=-\dfrac R2=-1.50\,\mathrm{cm}$. $\dfrac1{s'}=\dfrac1f-\dfrac1s=-\dfrac1{1.50}-\dfrac1{18.0}=-0.722\Rightarrow s'=-1.38\,\mathrm{cm}$: virtual, behind the mirror.
Worked example 2 · Refraction at a surface (Tutorial chapter 34, 34.21)
A swimmer $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the surface of a pool ($n=1.33$) sees the diving board directly overhead at an apparent height of $5.20\,\mathrm{m}$ ABOVE HER. What is the actual height of the board above the water?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $3.31\,\mathrm{m}$

The image is $5.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the swimmer, i.e. $5.20-0.80=4.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the SURFACE. Object in air seen from water: $h_{app}=\dfrac{n_{water}}{n_{air}}h\Rightarrow h=\dfrac{4.40}{1.33}=3.31\,\mathrm{m}$ (things above the surface look higher to a swimmer).
Worked example 3 · Thin lenses (Tutorial chapter 34, 34.32)
A converging lens of focal length $70.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ forms an image $4.50\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall, inverted, of a real object $3.20\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall. How far is the object from the lens?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $120\,\mathrm{cm}$

Inverted real image: $m=-\dfrac{4.50}{3.20}=-1.406=-\dfrac{s'}{s}\Rightarrow s'=1.406\,s$. $\dfrac1s+\dfrac1{1.406s}=\dfrac1{70.0}\Rightarrow\dfrac{1.711}{s}=\dfrac{1}{70.0}\Rightarrow s=120\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $s'=168\,\mathrm{cm}$ (real).
Worked example 4 · Plane and spherical mirrors (Tutorial chapter 34, 34.8)
For that convex mirror ($f=-1.50\,\mathrm{cm}$) and an object $18.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ away, what is the lateral magnification?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.0769$

$m=-\dfrac{s'}{s}=-\dfrac{-1.385}{18.0}=+0.0769$: erect and much smaller (a convex mirror always gives a diminished, upright, virtual image).
