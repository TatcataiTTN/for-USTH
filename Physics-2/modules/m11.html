Single-slit diffraction

Dark fringes at $a\sin\theta=m\lambda$ ($m=\pm1,\pm2,\dots$); the central maximum is twice as wide as the others.

Key formulas
$a\sin\theta=m\lambda$, $\ w_{central}=\dfrac{2\lambda L}{a}$, $\ I=I_0\left(\dfrac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2$, $\ \beta=\dfrac{\pi a\sin\theta}{\lambda}$

How it is asked: Slit width from the central maximum; Intensity at a point.

Single-slit diffraction: method and traps

Method
  1. Slit width $a$ (not slit separation).
  2. Minima: $a\sin\theta=m\lambda$; small angles $y=m\lambda L/a$.
  3. Intensity at a point: compute $\beta$ in radians, then $(\sin\beta/\beta)^2$.
Watch out
  • Forgetting the factor 2 for the full width of the central maximum.

Diffraction gratings

Principal maxima at $d\sin\theta=m\lambda$ with $d=1/N_{lines/length}$; resolving power $R=\lambda/\Delta\lambda=Nm$.

Key formulas
$d\sin\theta=m\lambda$, $\ d=\dfrac{1}{\text{lines per cm}}$, $\ m_{max}=\left\lfloor\dfrac d\lambda\right\rfloor$, dispersion $D=\dfrac{m}{d\cos\theta}$

How it is asked: Angle of the fourth-order maximum; Wavelength from the first-order angle.

Diffraction gratings: method and traps

Method
  1. Slit spacing $d$ from lines per unit length.
  2. Solve $\sin\theta=m\lambda/d$ (must be $\le1$).
  3. Highest order: largest $m$ with $m\lambda<d$.
Watch out
  • Lines per cm → $d$ in metres (e.g. 5000/cm → $2\times10^{-6}$ m).

Circular aperture and Rayleigh's criterion

Two point sources are just resolved when $\theta_R=1.22\lambda/D$.

Key formulas
$\theta_R=1.22\dfrac{\lambda}{D}$, $\ \theta=\dfrac{s}{L}$ (small angles), $\ D_{min}=\dfrac{1.22\lambda L}{s}$

How it is asked: Smallest dish to resolve two satellites; Resolution of the eye.

Circular aperture and Rayleigh's criterion: method and traps

Method
  1. Angular separation $\theta=s/L$ (radians).
  2. Set $\theta\ge1.22\lambda/D$ and isolate $D$ or $s$.
  3. Use 1.22 for circles, 1 for slits.
Watch out
  • Using the slit criterion $\lambda/a$ for a circular dish.

X-ray diffraction and Bragg's law

Constructive reflection from crystal planes spaced $d$: $2d\sin\theta=m\lambda$ (angle measured from the planes).

Key formulas
$2d\sin\theta=m\lambda$

How it is asked: Crystal plane spacing.

X-ray diffraction and Bragg's law: method and traps

Method
  1. Glancing angle from the planes, not from the normal.
  2. Isolate $d$ or $\lambda$.
  3. Largest order: $m<2d/\lambda$.
Watch out
  • Angle convention differs from gratings.

Combined interference and diffraction

Real double slits show the double-slit fringes inside the single-slit envelope; missing orders occur where $d/a$ is a whole number.

Key formulas
$I=I_m\cos^2\alpha\left(\dfrac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2$, $\ \alpha=\dfrac{\pi d\sin\theta}{\lambda}$, $\ \beta=\dfrac{\pi a\sin\theta}{\lambda}$, bright fringes inside the central envelope: $2\dfrac da-1$

How it is asked: Fringes inside the central diffraction peak.

Combined interference and diffraction: method and traps

Method
  1. Compute the fringe positions with $d$ and the envelope minima with $a$.
  2. A bright fringe that coincides with an envelope minimum is missing.
  3. Count the fringes inside the central envelope.
Watch out
  • Slit separation $d$ and slit width $a$ are different quantities.

Single-slit diffraction

$I=I_0\,[\sin\beta/\beta]^2$ with $\beta=\pi a\sin\theta/\lambda$; minima at $a\sin\theta=m\lambda$ ($m=\pm1,\pm2,\dots$). A narrower slit gives a wider central maximum, whose full width is $2\lambda/a$ in $\sin\theta$; the side maxima are only $4.7\,\%$ and $1.65\,\%$ of the central one.

$$I=I_0\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2,\quad \beta=\frac{\pi a\sin\theta}{\lambda},\quad a\sin\theta=m\lambda\ (\text{minima})$$

Double slit with slits of finite width: interference × diffraction

$d=3a$: the narrow interference fringes ($\cos^2$) sit under the single-slit envelope ($\mathrm{sinc}^2$). The orders $m=\pm3,\pm6,\dots$ fall exactly on the zeros of the envelope and are missing; 5 fringes fit in the central maximum.

$$I=I_0\cos^2\!\Big(\frac{\pi d\sin\theta}{\lambda}\Big)\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2,\qquad \text{missing orders } m=n\frac da$$

Diffraction gratings: more slits, sharper maxima

Intensity for $N=2$, $5$ and $20$ slits of the same spacing $d$. The principal maxima ($d\sin\theta=m\lambda$) stay where they are but their width shrinks as $1/N$, and $N-2$ weak secondary maxima appear between them.

$$I=I_0\left[\frac{\sin(N\varphi)}{N\sin\varphi}\right]^2,\qquad \varphi=\frac{\pi d\sin\theta}{\lambda}$$

Circular aperture: the Airy pattern and the Rayleigh criterion

$I=I_0\,[2J_1(x)/x]^2$ with $x=\pi D\sin\theta/\lambda$; the first dark ring is at $x=3.832$, i.e. $\sin\theta=1.22\lambda/D$. Two equal incoherent sources that far apart are just resolved: the dip between their peaks is about $26\,\%$.

$$I=I_0\left[\frac{2J_1(x)}{x}\right]^2,\qquad \theta_{\min}\approx1.22\frac{\lambda}{D}$$

Bragg reflection of X-rays

Crystal planes $0.282\,\mathrm{nm}$ apart: constructive reflection when $2d\sin\theta=m\lambda$. For $\lambda=0.154\,\mathrm{nm}$ the first order is at $15.8^\circ$; shorter wavelengths give smaller angles and higher orders appear when $\lambda\le2d/m$.

$$2d\sin\theta=m\lambda,\qquad m_{\max}=\left\lfloor\frac{2d}{\lambda}\right\rfloor$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Single-slit diffraction (Tutorial chapter 36, 36.6(a))
A tsunami has wave crests $1.0\,\mathrm{h}$ apart and speed $800\,\mathrm{km/h}$. What is its wavelength?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $800\,\mathrm{km}$

$\lambda=vT=(800\,\mathrm{km/h})(1.0\,\mathrm{h})=800\,\mathrm{km}$.
Worked example 2 · Combined interference and diffraction (Tutorial chapter 36, 36.19(a))
In a double-slit experiment the central diffraction maximum contains exactly seven interference fringes when $d/a=4$. What must $d/a$ be if the central maximum contains exactly FIVE fringes?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $3$

The central diffraction maximum ends at $a\sin\theta=\lambda$, where the interference order is $m=\dfrac da$. The orders strictly inside are $m=-(d/a-1),\dots,+(d/a-1)$: $2\dfrac da-1$ fringes. $7=2(4)-1$ ✓; for five fringes $2\dfrac da-1=5\Rightarrow\dfrac da=3$.
Worked example 3 · Diffraction gratings (Tutorial chapter 36, 36.33(a))
A wavelength of $656.45\,\mathrm{nm}$ in hydrogen and $656.27\,\mathrm{nm}$ in deuterium must be resolved in SECOND order. What minimum number of slits is needed? ($R=\lambda/\Delta\lambda=Nm$)
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $1820$

Resolving power $R=\dfrac{\lambda}{\Delta\lambda}=Nm\Rightarrow N=\dfrac{656.36}{(0.18)(2)}=1.82\times10^{3}$ slits.
Worked example 4 · Circular apertures and resolution (Tutorial chapter 36, 36.42(a))
A telephoto lens of focal length $135\,\mathrm{mm}$ is opened to $f/4.00$ (aperture diameter $f/N$). Photographing a bear $11.5\,\mathrm{m}$ away with $550\,\mathrm{nm}$ light, what is the width of the smallest resolvable feature on the bear?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $2.3\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}$

Aperture $D=\dfrac{135\,\mathrm{mm}}{4.00}=33.8\,\mathrm{mm}$. Resolution: $s=R\,\theta=R\,\dfrac{1.22\lambda}{D}=\dfrac{(11.5)(1.22)(550\times10^{-9})}{0.0338}=2.3\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}=0.23\,\mathrm{mm}$.
