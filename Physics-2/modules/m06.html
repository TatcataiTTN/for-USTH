Biot–Savart law

$d\vec B=\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{I\,d\vec s\times\hat r}{r^2}$: each current element contributes; integrate with symmetry.

Key formulas
$dB=\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{I\,ds\sin\theta}{r^2}$, finite straight wire $B=\dfrac{\mu_0I}{4\pi d}(\sin\theta_2-\sin\theta_1)$

How it is asked: Field of a finite segment; Field of an arc.

Biot–Savart law: method and traps

Method
  1. Sketch the element $d\vec s$, the vector $\hat r$ and the field point.
  2. Components that cancel by symmetry drop out.
  3. Integrate over the length or angle.
Watch out
  • Straight segments on the line through the point give zero.

Magnetic field of a long straight wire

$B=\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$, circling the wire by the right-hand rule.

Key formulas
$B=\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$, $\ \dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi}=2\times10^{-7}$ T·m/A

How it is asked: Field at a distance from a wire; Point of zero field between two wires.

Magnetic field of a long straight wire: method and traps

Method
  1. Distance $r$ in metres.
  2. Several wires: add the field vectors (direction by right-hand rule at the point).
  3. Zero point of two antiparallel currents lies outside, beside the smaller current.
Watch out
  • Mixing $\mu_0/2\pi r$ (wire) with $\mu_0/2R$ (loop centre).

Magnetic field of a circular loop and coil

At the centre $B=\mu_0I/2R$ (times $N$ for a coil); on the axis $B=\mu_0NIR^2/2(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}$.

Key formulas
$B_{centre}=\dfrac{\mu_0NI}{2R}$, $\ B_{axis}=\dfrac{\mu_0NIR^2}{2(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}}$, arc of angle $\phi$: $\dfrac{\mu_0I\phi}{4\pi R}$

How it is asked: Field at the centre of a loop; Two coaxial loops.

Magnetic field of a circular loop and coil: method and traps

Method
  1. Radius in metres and the number of turns $N$.
  2. Centre: $\mu_0NI/2R$; on the axis use the full formula.
  3. Arcs: fraction $\phi/2\pi$ of the loop.
Watch out
  • Direction: right-hand rule along the axis.

Ampère's law

$\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec s=\mu_0I_{enc}$: choose an Amperian loop of constant $B$ along its length.

Key formulas
wire: $B=\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$; inside a solid wire $B=\dfrac{\mu_0Ir}{2\pi R^2}$; hollow conductor: $B=0$ inside the hollow; coaxial cable

How it is asked: Field inside a hollow cylinder; Field of a coaxial cable.

Ampère's law: method and traps

Method
  1. Draw the Amperian loop from the symmetry.
  2. Count the current enclosed (partial for a current density).
  3. $B\cdot L_{loop}=\mu_0I_{enc}$, isolate $B$.
Watch out
  • Only the enclosed current counts; the field is not zero just because $I_{enc}=0$ unless the symmetry forces it.

Solenoids and toroids

Ideal solenoid: $B=\mu_0nI$ inside, $0$ outside; toroid: $B=\mu_0NI/2\pi r$ inside the windings.

Key formulas
$B=\mu_0nI$ ($n=N/L$), end of a long solenoid $\tfrac12\mu_0nI$, toroid $B=\dfrac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r}\propto\dfrac1r$

How it is asked: Solenoid field; Field inside a toroid.

Solenoids and toroids: method and traps

Method
  1. Turns per metre $n=N/L$ (not $N$).
  2. Inside: $\mu_0nI$; outside: zero (ideal).
  3. Toroid: field varies as $1/r$, zero outside.
Watch out
  • $N$ versus $n$ gives a factor of the length.

Gauss's law for magnetism

$\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec A=0$: there are no magnetic monopoles; field lines form closed loops.

Key formulas
net magnetic flux through any closed surface is $0$

How it is asked: Missing flux through a face.

Gauss's law for magnetism: method and traps

Method
  1. Closed surface ⇒ flux in equals flux out.
  2. Use it to find the missing flux through one face.
Watch out
  • Unlike electric flux, nothing is enclosed.

Magnetic field inside and outside a long straight wire

$I=10\,\mathrm{A}$ spread uniformly over a wire of radius $2\,\mathrm{mm}$: inside $B=\mu_0Ir/(2\pi R^2)$ rises linearly, outside $B=\mu_0I/(2\pi r)$ falls as $1/r$; the peak $1\,\mathrm{mT}$ is at the surface.

$$\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec\ell=\mu_0I_{\rm enc}\ \Rightarrow\ B=\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}\ (r>R)$$

A single loop against a Helmholtz pair

Two identical loops of radius $R$ a distance $R$ apart give an almost uniform field near the centre: the first and second derivatives of $B$ vanish there, so the variation within $\pm0.2R$ is under $0.5\,\%$ (a single loop varies by about $10\,\%$).

$$B_{\rm loop}(z)=\frac{\mu_0IR^2}{2(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}},\qquad B_{\rm pair}=B_{\rm loop}(z-\tfrac R2)+B_{\rm loop}(z+\tfrac R2)$$

Field on the axis of a finite solenoid

$n=2000$ turns per metre, $I=1.5\,\mathrm{A}$, length $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, radius $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the middle $B\approx\mu_0nI=3.77\,\mathrm{mT}$ (the ideal value); at each end it falls to about half of that.

$$B=\frac{\mu_0nI}{2}\left(\cos\theta_1-\cos\theta_2\right),\qquad B_{\rm ideal}=\mu_0nI$$

Force between parallel currents

$F/L=\mu_0I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$: inversely proportional to the separation (slope $-1$ on log-log axes) and proportional to the product of the currents; $1.0\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N/m}$ for $10\,\mathrm{A}$ and $10\,\mathrm{A}$ at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. Parallel currents attract.

$$\frac FL=\frac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Magnetic field of a loop (Magnetism tutoring slides, Example 6.2.2(b))
For the same coil ($100$ turns, $R=0.60\,\mathrm{m}$, $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$), what is the field on the axis $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ from the centre?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $1.1\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$

On the axis: $B=\dfrac{N\mu_0IR^2}{2(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}=\dfrac{100(4\pi\times10^{-7})(5.0)(0.36)}{2(0.36+0.64)^{3/2}}=1.1\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$. Check by summing Biot-Savart contributions numerically: same value. At $z\gg R$ this tends to the dipole form $\dfrac{N\mu_0IR^2}{2z^3}$.
Worked example 2 · Magnetic field of a straight wire (Magnetism tutoring slides, Example 6.2.1)
At what distance from a long straight wire carrying $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$ is the magnetic field $4.0\,\mu\mathrm{T}$?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$

$r=\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi B}=\dfrac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(1.0)}{2\pi(4.0\times10^{-6})}=0.050\,\mathrm{m}$.
Worked example 3 · Biot-Savart law (Magnetism tutoring slides, magnetic field from an arc of current)
A wire carrying $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ is bent into a quarter circle of radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ (the straight leads point towards the centre and contribute nothing). What is the field at the centre of the arc?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $2.4\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}$

An arc of angle $\varphi$ gives the fraction $\dfrac{\varphi}{2\pi}$ of a full loop: $B=\dfrac{\mu_0I\varphi}{4\pi R}=\dfrac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(3.0)(\pi/2)}{4\pi(0.20)}=2.4\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}$ (verified by direct numerical summation).
Worked example 4 · Solenoids and toroids (Magnetism tutoring slides, Young and Freedman 28.42)
A $15.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ long solenoid of radius $0.750\,\mathrm{cm}$ is closely wound with $600$ turns and carries $8.00\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the magnetic field near its centre?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.0402\,\mathrm{T}$

$B=\mu_0nI$ with $n=\dfrac NL=\dfrac{600}{0.150}=4000\,\mathrm{turns/m}$: $B=(4\pi\times10^{-7})(4000)(8.00)=4.02\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{T}$ (the radius does not matter for a long solenoid).
