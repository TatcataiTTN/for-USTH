Magnetic flux

$\Phi_B=\int\vec B\cdot d\vec A=BA\cos\theta$; unit weber (T·m²).

Key formulas
$\Phi_B=BA\cos\theta$, $\ 1\ \mathrm{Wb}=1\ \mathrm{T\,m^2}$, $\ N$ turns: $N\Phi_B$

How it is asked: Flux through a loop.

Magnetic flux: method and traps

Method
  1. $\theta$ is between $\vec B$ and the normal to the loop.
  2. Area of a circle $\pi r^2$ (radius in metres).
  3. Flux changes if $B$, $A$ or $\theta$ changes.
Watch out
  • Maximum flux when the loop face is perpendicular to $\vec B$.

Faraday's law of induction

$\mathcal E=-N\,d\Phi_B/dt$: the emf equals the rate of change of flux (times the number of turns).

Key formulas
$|\mathcal E|=NA\left|\dfrac{dB}{dt}\right|$ (area fixed), $\ I=\dfrac{\mathcal E}{R}$, $\ q=\dfrac{N\Delta\Phi}{R}$

How it is asked: Emf from a time-varying field; Emf in a rotating coil.

Faraday's law of induction: method and traps

Method
  1. Write $\Phi_B(t)$ for the loop.
  2. Differentiate in $t$ first, then substitute the time.
  3. Current from $I=\mathcal E/R$; direction from Lenz.
Watch out
  • Substituting $t$ into $B$ before differentiating.

Lenz's law

The induced current opposes the change of flux that produces it.

Key formulas
flux increasing ⇒ induced field opposes $\vec B$; flux decreasing ⇒ induced field adds to $\vec B$

How it is asked: Direction of the induced current; Reduce eddy-current drag.

Lenz's law: method and traps

Method
  1. Decide whether the flux is increasing or decreasing.
  2. Induced field opposes the change; right-hand rule gives the current.
  3. Eddy-current braking is Lenz's law at work.
Watch out
  • Opposes the change, not the field itself.

Motional emf

A conductor of length $L$ moving at $v$ across $B$ has emf $\mathcal E=BLv$.

Key formulas
$\mathcal E=BLv$, $\ I=\dfrac{BLv}{R}$, $\ F=\dfrac{B^2L^2v}{R}$ (magnetic drag), $\ P=\dfrac{B^2L^2v^2}{R}$

How it is asked: Rod sliding on rails; Emf of an airplane wing.

Motional emf: method and traps

Method
  1. $\mathcal E=BLv$ for perpendicular $\vec v$, $\vec B$, rod.
  2. Current $I=\mathcal E/R$; force $ILB$ opposes the motion.
  3. Power dissipated equals the mechanical power pushing the rod.
Watch out
  • Which side is positive follows from $\vec v\times\vec B$ on positive charges.

Induced electric fields and eddy currents

A changing magnetic flux creates a non-conservative electric field: $\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec s=-d\Phi_B/dt$.

Key formulas
$E\cdot2\pi r=\pi r^2\left|\dfrac{dB}{dt}\right|\ \Rightarrow\ E=\dfrac r2\left|\dfrac{dB}{dt}\right|$ (inside a uniform changing $B$)

How it is asked: Induced field inside a solenoid region.

Induced electric fields and eddy currents: method and traps

Method
  1. Take a circle of radius $r$ concentric with the field region.
  2. Equate $E\cdot2\pi r$ with the rate of flux change through the circle.
  3. Slots or lamination interrupt eddy-current paths.
Watch out
  • Such fields do not come from charges and have closed lines.

Inductance and energy in an inductor

$L=N\Phi_B/I$; the self-induced emf is $-L\,dI/dt$; the stored energy is $\tfrac12LI^2$.

Key formulas
$L=\dfrac{N\Phi_B}{I}$, solenoid $L=\mu_0n^2\ell A$, $\ U_B=\tfrac12LI^2$, $\ u_B=\dfrac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$, unit henry

How it is asked: Inductance of a solenoid; Energy stored in the field.

Inductance and energy in an inductor: method and traps

Method
  1. Flux per turn from the field of the geometry, then $L=N\Phi/I$.
  2. Self-emf from $dI/dt$.
  3. Energy $\tfrac12LI^2$ or energy density $B^2/2\mu_0$ times volume.
Watch out
  • Inductance depends on geometry only, not on the current.

RL circuits

Current grows as $I=\frac{\mathcal E}{R}(1-e^{-t/\tau})$ and decays as $I_0e^{-t/\tau}$ with $\tau=L/R$.

Key formulas
$\tau=\dfrac LR$, $\ I(t)=\dfrac{\mathcal E}{R}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$, $\ I(t)=I_0e^{-t/\tau}$ (decay), at $t=\tau$: $63\%$

How it is asked: Time for the current to reach half.

RL circuits: method and traps

Method
  1. Time constant $\tau=L/R$.
  2. Growth or decay formula; isolate $t$ with a logarithm.
  3. At $t=0$ the inductor acts like an open circuit, at $t\to\infty$ like a wire.
Watch out
  • Use $\ln$ to solve for time.

Faraday's law: flux and induced emf

A coil of $N=200$ turns in a field whose flux through one turn is $\Phi=\Phi_0\sin\omega t$ ($\Phi_0=2\,\mathrm{mWb}$, $f=50\,\mathrm{Hz}$). The emf $-N\,d\Phi/dt$ is a cosine: largest when the flux is zero and zero when the flux is at its peak; its amplitude is $N\omega\Phi_0=126\,\mathrm{V}$.

$$\varepsilon=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}$$

Growth and decay of the current in an RL circuit

$\varepsilon=12\,\mathrm{V}$, $R=24\,\Omega$, $L=0.60\,\mathrm{H}$: $\tau=L/R=25\,\mathrm{ms}$. The current reaches $63\,\%$ of $\varepsilon/R=0.5\,\mathrm{A}$ at $t=\tau$ and is $37\,\%$ of its initial value one time constant after the battery is removed. The stored energy is $\tfrac12LI^2$.

$$I=\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\qquad I=I_0e^{-t/\tau},\qquad \tau=\frac LR$$

Motional emf: a sliding rod is braked by its own induced current

A rod ($m=0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$, $l=0.40\,\mathrm{m}$) slides on rails closed through $R=0.30\,\Omega$ in $B=0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ with initial speed $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The magnetic force $B^2l^2v/R$ opposes the motion: $v=v_0e^{-t/\tau}$ with $\tau=mR/(B^2l^2)=0.75\,\mathrm{s}$; all the initial kinetic energy ends as heat in $R$.

$$\varepsilon=Blv,\quad m\frac{dv}{dt}=-\frac{B^2l^2}{R}v,\quad \tau=\frac{mR}{B^2l^2}$$

Energy stored in an inductor

$U=\tfrac12LI^2$ grows with the square of the current and in proportion to the inductance. The same coil that stores $0.075\,\mathrm{J}$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{A}$ ($L=0.6\,\mathrm{H}$) stores four times as much at $1\,\mathrm{A}$.

$$U=\tfrac12LI^2=\int_0^I Li\,di$$

Induced electric field of a changing solenoid field

$R=5\,\mathrm{cm}$, $dB/dt=0.020\,\mathrm{T/s}$: inside the solenoid $E=\tfrac r2\,dB/dt$ grows linearly ($3.0\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$ at $3\,\mathrm{cm}$); outside it falls as $R^2/(2r)\,dB/dt$ ($2.5\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$ at $10\,\mathrm{cm}$), although $B=0$ there.

$$E\,(2\pi r)=\pi r_{\rm eff}^2\,\frac{dB}{dt}$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Faraday's law (Final tutoring slides 2025-26 (induction), Faraday's law of induction)
A square loop of side $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has half of its area in a uniform field perpendicular to it. The loop contains an ideal battery of emf $20.0\,\mathrm{V}$. The field varies as $B=0.0420-0.870\,t$ (T, s). If the induced emf has the same sense as the battery's, what is the net emf in the circuit?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $21.7\,\mathrm{V}$

Flux through the loop: $\Phi=B\cdot\dfrac{A_{\text{loop}}}{2}=B(2.00\,\mathrm{m^2})$, so $|\varepsilon_{\text{ind}}|=2.00\left|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|=2.00(0.870)=1.74\,\mathrm{V}$ (the field decreases, so the induced current tries to maintain the flux). Net emf $=20.0+1.74=21.7\,\mathrm{V}$ (it would be $18.3\,\mathrm{V}$ if the induced emf opposed the battery).
Worked example 2 · Lenz's law (Final tutoring slides 2025-26 (induction), Lenz's law)
A uniform field points INTO the page and its magnitude is decreasing. The induced current in a circular loop in the plane of the page is
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: clockwise

The inward flux decreases, so the induced current produces an inward field of its own to oppose the decrease: by the right-hand rule a CLOCKWISE current gives a field into the page inside the loop.
Worked example 3 · Faraday's law (Final tutoring slides 2025-26 (induction), Faraday's law of induction)
A flat coil with $200$ turns and area $0.05\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is in a uniform field perpendicular to its plane. The field changes from $0$ to $1.5\,\mathrm{T}$ in $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the induced emf?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $5\,\mathrm{V}$

Faraday: $|\varepsilon|=N\left|\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}\right|=NA\dfrac{\Delta B}{\Delta t}=(200)(0.05)\dfrac{1.5}{3.0}=5.0\,\mathrm{V}$.
