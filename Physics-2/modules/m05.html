Magnetic force on a moving charge

$\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec B$: perpendicular to both $\vec v$ and $\vec B$, so it does no work.

Key formulas
$F=|q|vB\sin\theta$, direction by the right-hand rule, reversed for negative charges, $\ \vec F=q\vec E+q\vec v\times\vec B$

How it is asked: Direction of the force on an electron; Work done by a magnetic field.

Magnetic force on a moving charge: method and traps

Method
  1. Cross product $\vec v\times\vec B$ by components or the right-hand rule.
  2. Negative charge ⇒ flip the direction.
  3. No force if $\vec v\parallel\vec B$.
Watch out
  • Order in the cross product matters.

Circular motion in a magnetic field

For $\vec v\perp\vec B$ the path is a circle: $r=mv/|q|B$, period $T=2\pi m/|q|B$ (independent of speed).

Key formulas
$r=\dfrac{mv}{|q|B}=\dfrac{p}{|q|B}$, $\ \omega=\dfrac{|q|B}{m}$, $\ T=\dfrac{2\pi m}{|q|B}$, $\ K=\dfrac{(qBr)^2}{2m}$; helix pitch $=v_\parallel T$

How it is asked: Radius of an orbit; Cyclotron period; Mass spectrometer.

Circular motion in a magnetic field: method and traps

Method
  1. Magnetic force supplies the centripetal force: $|q|vB=mv^2/r$.
  2. Isolate $r$, $v$ or $B$; for energy gained from $V$: $qV=\tfrac12mv^2$ first.
  3. Component along $B$ is unchanged (helical path).
Watch out
  • Mass in kg (proton $1.67\times10^{-27}$ kg, electron $9.11\times10^{-31}$ kg).

Crossed fields and the velocity selector

A charge moves undeflected when $qE=qvB$, i.e. $v=E/B$.

Key formulas
$v=\dfrac{E}{B}$, $\ \vec F_{net}=q(\vec E+\vec v\times\vec B)$

How it is asked: Speed that passes undeflected; Thomson e/m.

Crossed fields and the velocity selector: method and traps

Method
  1. Make the electric and magnetic forces opposite and equal.
  2. Isolate $v=E/B$ (independent of $q$ and $m$).
  3. Faster or slower particles are deflected.
Watch out
  • The speed that passes is independent of the particle's charge.

Hall effect

A current in a magnetic field builds a transverse Hall voltage until $qE_H=qv_dB$; it reveals the sign and density of the carriers.

Key formulas
$V_H=v_dBw$, $\ n=\dfrac{IB}{e\,t\,V_H}$, $\ v_d=\dfrac{J}{ne}$

How it is asked: Hall voltage of a strip; Carrier density from the Hall voltage.

Hall effect: method and traps

Method
  1. Force balance $eE_H=ev_dB$.
  2. $V_H=E_Hw$ with $w$ the width across the Hall field.
  3. Sign of $V_H$ gives the sign of the carriers.
Watch out
  • Width $w$ is across the field, thickness $t$ along $B$.

Force on a current-carrying wire

$\vec F=I\vec L\times\vec B$, magnitude $ILB\sin\theta$.

Key formulas
$F=BIL\sin\theta$, curved wire: $\vec F=I\vec L_{chord}\times\vec B$

How it is asked: Force on a wire segment; Wire balanced against gravity.

Force on a current-carrying wire: method and traps

Method
  1. Direction of $\vec L$ along the current.
  2. Magnitude $BIL\sin\theta$ with the length inside the field.
  3. Equilibrium (wire on rails or a hanging wire): set $F=mg\tan\phi$ or $BIL=mg$.
Watch out
  • Only the segment inside the field counts.

Torque on a current loop and magnetic moment

A loop has magnetic moment $\mu=NIA$ (right-hand rule); in a field the torque is $\tau=\mu B\sin\theta$ and the energy $U=-\vec\mu\cdot\vec B$.

Key formulas
$\vec\mu=NI\vec A$, $\ \vec\tau=\vec\mu\times\vec B$, $\ U=-\mu B\cos\theta$ (minimum when aligned: stable)

How it is asked: Torque on a rectangular coil; Energy of a dipole.

Torque on a current loop and magnetic moment: method and traps

Method
  1. Compute $\mu=NIA$ and its direction.
  2. Angle $\theta$ between $\vec\mu$ and $\vec B$.
  3. Work to rotate: $\Delta U=\mu B(\cos\theta_i-\cos\theta_f)$.
Watch out
  • The field exerts a torque but no net force on a loop in a uniform field.

Force between parallel currents

Parallel currents attract, antiparallel repel: $F/L=\mu_0I_1I_2/2\pi d$.

Key formulas
$\dfrac FL=\dfrac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}$, $\ \mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T·m/A

How it is asked: Force per metre between two wires.

Force between parallel currents: method and traps

Method
  1. Field of wire 1 at wire 2: $B=\mu_0I_1/2\pi d$.
  2. Force on wire 2: $F=I_2LB$.
  3. Direction: same direction attract.
Watch out
  • Equal and opposite forces on the two wires (Newton 3).

Charged particles in a uniform magnetic field

$B=0.05\,\mathrm{T}$ into the page, both particles at $10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$: a proton goes round a circle of radius $mv/(eB)=2.1\,\mathrm{cm}$; an alpha particle (twice the charge, four times the mass) a circle twice as big, in the same sense. The period $2\pi m/(qB)$ does not depend on the speed.

$$r=\frac{mv}{|q|B},\qquad T=\frac{2\pi m}{|q|B},\qquad f=\frac{|q|B}{2\pi m}$$

Helical motion: velocity components along and across the field

An electron with $v_\perp=3\times10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_\parallel=1\times10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $B=2\,\mathrm{mT}$: a circle of radius $r=mv_\perp/(eB)$ in the plane perpendicular to $\vec B$ combined with uniform motion along $\vec B$; the pitch is $p=v_\parallel T$.

$$r=\frac{mv_\perp}{eB},\qquad p=v_\parallel T=\frac{2\pi mv_\parallel}{eB}$$

Hall voltage against the magnetic field

A strip $0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ thick carrying $10\,\mathrm{A}$: $V_H=IB/(net)$ is linear in $B$, and tiny for a metal (copper) but thousands of times larger for a semiconductor with few carriers; that is why Hall probes use semiconductors.

$$V_H=\frac{IB}{n\,e\,t}$$

Torque and potential energy of a current loop in a field

A coil with $\mu=NIA=2\,\mathrm{A\,m^2}$ in $B=0.30\,\mathrm{T}$: the torque $\tau=\mu B\sin\phi$ is largest ($0.6\,\mathrm{N\,m}$) when the plane is parallel to $\vec B$; the potential energy $U=-\mu B\cos\phi$ is lowest when $\vec\mu\parallel\vec B$ (the torque is the slope of $U$, $\tau=dU/d\phi$).

$$\vec\tau=\vec\mu\times\vec B,\qquad U=-\vec\mu\cdot\vec B,\qquad \mu=NIA$$

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Crossed fields (Magnetism tutoring slides, Question 7)
An electron has initial velocity $(12.0\,\hat j+15.0\,\hat k)\,\mathrm{km/s}$ and constant acceleration $(2.00\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2})\hat i$ in a region of uniform fields. If $\vec B=(400\,\mu\mathrm{T})\,\hat i$, what is the electric field $\vec E$?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $(-11.4\,\hat i-6\,\hat j+4.8\,\hat k)\,\mathrm{N/C}$

Newton's second law: $m\vec a=q(\vec E+\vec v\times\vec B)$ with $q=-e$, so $\vec E=-\dfrac me\vec a-\vec v\times\vec B$. $\vec v\times\vec B=(0,\ 6.00,\ -4.80)\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and $-\dfrac{m}{e}\vec a=-11.4\,\hat i\,\mathrm{N/C}$. $\vec E=(-11.4\,\hat i-6.00\,\hat j+4.80\,\hat k)\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
Worked example 2 · Force on a current-carrying wire (Magnetism tutoring slides, Question 2(b))
For the same line (current towards the north, Earth's field towards the north and downwards), the direction of the magnetic force is
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: west (horizontal)

$\vec F=I\vec L\times\vec B$. With north $=\hat y$, east $=\hat x$, up $=\hat z$: $\vec B\propto(\cos70^\circ\hat y-\sin70^\circ\hat z)$ and $\hat y\times(-\hat z)=-\hat x$: the force points west.
Worked example 3 · Torque on a current loop (Magnetism tutoring slides, Question 3)
A circular loop of radius $8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries a current of $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$. Its magnetic moment points along the unit vector $0.60\,\hat i-0.80\,\hat j$. The loop is in a uniform field $\vec B=(0.25\,\hat i+0.30\,\hat k)\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the magnitude of the torque on the loop?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $0.00145\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

$\mu=IA=I\pi r^2=(0.20)\pi(0.080)^2=4.02\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{A\,m^2}$, so $\vec\mu=(2.41\times10^{-3}\hat i-3.22\times10^{-3}\hat j)\,\mathrm{A\,m^2}$. $\vec\tau=\vec\mu\times\vec B=(-9.65\times10^{-4}\hat i-7.24\times10^{-4}\hat j+8.04\times10^{-4}\hat k)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, magnitude $1.45\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.
Worked example 4 · Magnetic force on a charge (Magnetism tutoring slides, Question 1)
An electron has velocity $\vec v=(2.0\times10^{6}\hat i+3.0\times10^{6}\hat j)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform field $\vec B=(0.030\,\hat i-0.15\,\hat j)\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on it?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $6.2\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}$

$\vec F=q\,\vec v\times\vec B$ with $q=-e$. Both vectors lie in the $xy$ plane, so the cross product points along $z$: $(\vec v\times\vec B)_z=v_xB_y-v_yB_x=(2.0\times10^6)(-0.15)-(3.0\times10^6)(0.030)=-3.9\times10^{5}\,\mathrm{T\,m/s}$. $|F|=e\,(3.9\times10^5)=6.2\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}$.
