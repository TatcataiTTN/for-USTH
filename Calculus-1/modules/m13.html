After this module you can

  • Decide absolute / conditional convergence of alternating series.
  • Find radius and interval of convergence (check both endpoints).
  • Build Taylor / Maclaurin series from known series and estimate the error.

Alternating series

  • $\sum(-1)^nb_n$ converges if $b_n\downarrow0$ (AST). Error after $N$ terms: $|R_N|\le b_{N+1}$.
  • Absolute: $\sum|a_n|$ converges (then $\sum a_n$ converges). Conditional: $\sum a_n$ converges but $\sum|a_n|$ diverges.
  • $\sum(-1)^n\frac{\ln n}{n-\ln n}$ is conditionally convergent; $\sum\frac{(-2)^{n+1}}{n+5^n}$ is absolutely convergent.
  • If $a_n\not\to0$ the series diverges: $\sum(-1)^{n+1}\left(\frac n{10}\right)^n$.

Radius and interval of convergence

  • $\sum c_n(x-a)^n$: $R=\lim\left|\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}\right|$ (or $\frac1{\limsup\sqrt[n]{|c_n|}}$). Converges absolutely for $|x-a|<R$, diverges for $|x-a|>R$.
  • Always test the endpoints $x=a\pm R$ separately.
  • $\sum\frac{(x-1)^n}{\sqrt n}$: $R=1$; $x=2$ diverges, $x=0$ converges: interval $[0,2)$.
  • $\sum n^nx^n$: $R=0$. $\sum\frac{x^n}{n!}$: $R=\infty$.

Taylor and Maclaurin series

FunctionMaclaurin seriesConverges
$e^x$$\sum\frac{x^n}{n!}$all $x$
$\sin x$$\sum\frac{(-1)^nx^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$all $x$
$\cos x$$\sum\frac{(-1)^nx^{2n}}{(2n)!}$all $x$
$\frac1{1-x}$$\sum x^n$$|x|<1$
$\ln(1+x)$$\sum\frac{(-1)^{n+1}x^n}{n}$$-1<x\le1$

Taylor at $a$: $c_n=\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}$. Substitute and differentiate/integrate known series term by term.

Building series and bounding the error

  • $\frac{2x}{(1-x^2)^2}=\frac{d}{dx}\frac1{1-x^2}=\sum_{n\ge1}2nx^{2n-1}$. $\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}=2\left(x+\frac{x^3}3+\frac{x^5}5+\cdots\right)$.
  • $5\cos\pi x=\sum\frac{5(-1)^k(\pi x)^{2k}}{(2k)!}$; $2^x$ about $x=1$: $\sum\frac{2(\ln2)^n}{n!}(x-1)^n$.
  • Lagrange: $|R_n(x)|\le\frac{M|x-a|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$ with $M\ge|f^{(n+1)}|$ between $a$ and $x$.
  • $\ln1.1$ within $10^{-8}$: need $\frac{0.1^{n+1}}{n+1}<10^{-8}$, so $n=7$.

Typical mistakes

EndpointsForgetting to test $x=a\pm R$.

Error boundsUse $M$ = max of the NEXT derivative on the interval.

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Alternating series (Tutorial 12, Q2(a))
$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-2)^{n+1}}{n+5^n}$
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: converges absolutely

$\left|\frac{(-2)^{n+1}}{n+5^n}\right|\le2\left(\frac25\right)^n$, a convergent geometric series: comparison test ⇒ absolute convergence.
Worked example 2 · Power series (Tutorial 12, Q3(a))
$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{(x-1)^n}{\sqrt n}$ has radius $R$ and interval of convergence
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $R=1$, interval $[0,2)$

Ratio test: $|x-1|\lim\sqrt{\frac n{n+1}}=|x-1|<1$. At $x=2$: $\sum\frac1{\sqrt n}$ diverges; at $x=0$: $\sum\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}$ converges (AST). So $[0,2)$: absolutely for $0<x<2$, conditionally at $x=0$.
Worked example 3 · Taylor and Maclaurin series (Tutorial 12, Q4)
In the Taylor polynomial of $f(x)=\sin x$ about $a=\frac\pi4$, the coefficient of $\left(x-\frac\pi4\right)^2$ is
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{4}$

$c_2=\frac{f''(\pi/4)}{2!}=\frac{-\sin(\pi/4)}{2}=-\frac{\sqrt2}{4}$. The full cubic is $\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\left[1+u-\frac{u^2}2-\frac{u^3}6\right]$, $u=x-\frac\pi4$.
Worked example 4 · Alternating series (Tutorial 12, Q2(b))
$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty(-1)^n\dfrac{\ln n}{n-\ln n}$
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: converges conditionally (not absolutely)

$b_n=\frac{\ln n}{n-\ln n}\to0$ and is eventually decreasing, so the series converges by the AST. But $\sum b_n$ diverges (compare with $\frac{1}{n}$: $b_n\ge\frac1n$ for $n\ge3$), so the convergence is conditional.
