Continuity

  • $f$ is continuous at $a$ if: (1) $f(a)$ is defined, (2) $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists, (3) $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$.
  • Polynomials, rational functions (on their domain), roots, $\sin,\cos,e^x,\ln x$ are continuous; sums, products, quotients and compositions of continuous functions are continuous.
  • Piecewise function: continuity at the junction $\iff$ left limit = right limit = value.
  • Example: $x^2-1$ ($x<3$), $2ax$ ($x\ge3$) is continuous iff $8=6a$, i.e. $a=\frac43$.

infinitea one-sided limit is $\pm\infty$$\frac{1}{x^2}$
oscillatingno one-sided limit$\sin\frac1x$

Intermediate Value Theorem

  • If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $N$ is between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, then $f(c)=N$ for some $c\in(a,b)$.
  • Root finding: if $f(a)f(b)<0$ there is a root in $(a,b)$.
  • Example: $x^3-15x+1$ has $f(-4)=-3$, $f(-1)=15$, $f(1)=-13$, $f(4)=5$: three sign changes, three roots in $[-4,4]$.
  • Continuity on the whole closed interval is essential (a jump can skip the value).

Applications

  • $e^x=-x$ has a solution: $F(x)=e^x+x$ has $F(-1)<0<F(0)$.
  • $(1-x)^3=\sin x$ has a root in $(0,1)$: $F(0)=1>0$, $F(1)=-\sin1<0$.
  • The monk and the mountain: let $h(t)=$ (position going up) $-$ (position going down); $h(\text{start})<0<h(\text{end})$ so $h(t)=0$ at some time.
  • Bisection: halve the interval keeping the sign change (see the Calculus lab).

Typical mistakes

No sign change$f(a)f(b)>0$ does not prove there is no root (two roots may hide inside).
Forgetting closed intervalIVT needs continuity on all of $[a,b]$.
Product of continuous$fg$ continuous at $0$ does NOT force $f$ and $g$ to be continuous.

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Continuity (Computation problems 4, Q2)
Let $g(x)=x+b$ for $x<0$ and $g(x)=\cos x$ for $x\ge0$. Which statement is true?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $g$ is continuous at $0$ iff $b=1$, but $g$ is not differentiable at $0$ for any $b$

Continuity: $\lim_{x\to0^-}(x+b)=b$ must equal $\cos0=1$, so $b=1$. One-sided derivatives: left $=1$, right $=-\sin0=0$. They differ, so $g'(0)$ never exists.
Worked example 2 · Intermediate Value Theorem (Tutorial 3, Q5)
For $f(x)=x^3-15x+1$, on which interval do the hypotheses of the IVT guarantee a root (a sign change of the continuous function $f$)?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $[3,4]$

$f$ is continuous, so a root is guaranteed on $[a,b]$ when $f(a)f(b)<0$. Here $f(3)=-17$, $f(4)=5$ have opposite signs; for the other intervals the endpoint values have the same sign.
Worked example 3 · Continuity (Tutorial 2, Q8)
For which $a$ is $f(x)=x^2-1$ ($x<3$), $f(x)=2ax$ ($x\ge3$) continuous at every $x$?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $\frac{4}{3}$

Continuity at $3$: $\lim_{x\to3^-}(x^2-1)=8$ must equal $f(3)=6a$, so $a=\frac43$.
Worked example 4 · Intermediate Value Theorem (Tutorial 3, Q5)
For $f(x)=x^3-15x+1$, on which interval do the hypotheses of the IVT guarantee a root (a sign change of the continuous function $f$)?
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $[-4,-3]$

$f$ is continuous, so a root is guaranteed on $[a,b]$ when $f(a)f(b)<0$. Here $f(-4)=-3$, $f(-3)=19$ have opposite signs; for the other intervals the endpoint values have the same sign.
