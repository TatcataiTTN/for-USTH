Volumes: three methods

MethodFormulaUse when
cross-sections$V=\int A(x)\,dx$known shape of slices
disk / washer (about the $x$-axis)$\pi\int(R^2-r^2)\,dx$slices ⊥ axis of rotation
shells (about the $y$-axis)$2\pi\int x\,h(x)\,dx$slices ∥ axis of rotation

Radius of a shell = distance to the axis; for the axis $x=4$ it is $4-x$.

Worked setups

  • Disks with diameter from $y=x^2$ to $y=2-x^2$ on $[-1,1]$: $V=\pi\int(1-x^2)^2dx=\frac{16\pi}{15}$.
  • Triangle $2y=x+4$, $y=x$, $x=0$: about the $x$-axis (washers) $16\pi$; about the $y$-axis (shells) $\frac{32\pi}3$; about $x=4$ (shells) $\frac{64\pi}{3}$; about $y=8$ (washers) $48\pi$.
  • Region between $y=4-x^2$ and $y=2-x$ about the $x$-axis: $\pi\int_{-1}^2[(4-x^2)^2-(2-x)^2]dx=\frac{108\pi}5$.
  • Check with Pappus: $V=2\pi\cdot(\text{distance of the centroid})\cdot(\text{area})$.

Arc length and surface area

$$L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx,\qquad S=2\pi\int_a^bf(x)\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx$$
  • $y=\frac{x^3}3+\frac1{4x}$ on $[1,3]$: $1+y'^2=\left(x^2+\frac1{4x^2}\right)^2$, $L=\frac{53}6$.
  • $y=\int_0^x\tan t\,dt$: $\sqrt{1+\tan^2x}=\sec x$, $L=\ln\sqrt3$ on $[0,\frac\pi6]$.
  • Astroid $x^{2/3}+y^{2/3}=1$: total length $6$.

Typical mistakes

Wrong radiusRadius is the distance to the axis, not the coordinate.
Washer vs diskSubtract the squares $R^2-r^2$, not $(R-r)^2$.
Arc lengthLook for the perfect square under the root.

Worked examples from the course problems

Worked example 1 · Volumes by cross-sections (Tutorial 8, Q1)
A solid lies between $x=-1$ and $x=1$; its cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are disks with diameter from $y=x^2$ to $y=2-x^2$. Its volume is
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $\frac{16 \pi}{15}$

Diameter $d=2-2x^2$, radius $1-x^2$: $V=\pi\int_{-1}^1(1-x^2)^2dx=\pi\left(2-\frac43+\frac25\right)=\frac{16\pi}{15}$.
Worked example 2 · Volumes of revolution (Tutorial 8, Q6(a))
Revolve the triangle bounded by $2y=x+4$, $y=x$ and $x=0$ about the $x$-axis (washer method). Volume:
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $16 \pi$

$V=\pi\int_0^4\left[\left(\frac{x+4}{2}\right)^2-x^2\right]dx=\pi\int_0^4\left(4+2x-\frac34x^2\right)dx=16\pi$.
Worked example 3 · Arc length and surface area (Tutorial 8, Q7)
Find the length of $y=\dfrac{x^3}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4x}$ for $1\le x\le3$.
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $\frac{53}{6}$

$y'=x^2-\frac1{4x^2}$ and $1+y'^2=\left(x^2+\frac1{4x^2}\right)^2$, so $L=\int_1^3\left(x^2+\frac1{4x^2}\right)dx=\left[\frac{x^3}{3}-\frac1{4x}\right]_1^3=\frac{53}6$.
Worked example 4 · Volumes by cross-sections (Tutorial 8, Q2(a))
The base of a solid is the region under $y=2\sqrt{\sin x}$, $0\le x\le\pi$; cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are equilateral triangles with a side on the base. Volume:
Show the answer and the solution

Answer: $2 \sqrt{3}$

Side $s=2\sqrt{\sin x}$, area $\frac{\sqrt3}4s^2=\sqrt3\sin x$; $V=\sqrt3\int_0^\pi\sin x\,dx=2\sqrt3$.
